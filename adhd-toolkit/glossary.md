# ADHD Toolkit: Comprehensive Glossary

An alphabetical reference of core neurobiological, psychological, executive function, and nutritional terms, drawn from published clinical and educational material on ADHD and paraphrased in this skill's own words.

**Reading the tags.** `(Ch N)` refers to this skill's own chapter files. A bracketed
tag like `` `[verified]` `` marks a claim traceable to documented clinical or
educational material. Entries marked `[practitioner-common]` are genuine, widely used
ADHD concepts that are **not** tied to any single documented source — they are kept
because they are useful, and labeled so the distinction is honest. Full audit:
[sources.md](sources.md).

---

## A
- **Alpha Brain Waves (8–12 Hz)** `[practitioner-common]` — Brainwave frequency associated with relaxed alertness. Neither the band nor the L-theanine link is documented in this skill's sources (Ch 12).
- **Apigenin** — A calming plant bioflavonoid found in chamomile that binds to central benzodiazepine receptors to promote sleep onset (Ch 12).
- **Asynchronous Development** — Executive function in ADHD is delayed **up to 30 percent** relative to age peers `[verified]` (Ch 5).
- **Attentional Dysregulation** — The neurological difficulty in consciously allocating, sustaining, and shifting focus across tasks on demand (Ch 1, Ch 5).

## B
- **Backward Planning (Reverse Scheduling)** — Time management technique that begins at the hard deadline and maps backward, incorporating realistic transit and transition buffers (Ch 7).
- **BDNF (Brain-Derived Neurotrophic Factor)** — A protein released when you raise your heart rate that creates a fertile environment for growing new neurons, connectors, and positive pathways `[verified]`. Exercise also drives a boost in dopamine and norepinephrine, which play a major role in regulating the attention system `[verified]` (Ch 4).
- **Body Doubling** — Working in the physical or virtual presence of another person, providing passive social scaffolding that maintains task momentum (Ch 3, Ch 8).
- **Butyrate** — A short-chain fatty acid produced by gut bacteria. Valles-Colomer (2019) surveyed over a thousand people and found butyrate-producing bacteria were consistently linked to better self-reported quality of life `[verified]` (Ch 10). Documented sources do **not** claim it strengthens the blood-brain barrier or reduces neuroinflammation — that mechanism was asserted by an earlier version of this file.

## C
- **Casein (A1 Beta-Casein)** — The main dairy protein. In a 2016 trial (Sun Jianqin), subjects drinking A1-containing milk had more gastrointestinal inflammation, slower thinking, and more information-processing errors than on A2-only milk. Goat's and sheep's milk are generally A2 (Ch 11).
- **Cerebellum** — Occupies only 10% of brain volume yet contains 75% of the brain's neurons `[verified]`; coordinates motor balance as well as cognitive timing, sequencing, and emotional control (Ch 2).
- **Cognitive Flexibility** — The executive capacity to adapt behavior and strategy when faced with unexpected changes, new rules, or obstacles (Ch 5).
- **Cross-Lateral Integration** `[practitioner-common]` — Exercises where limbs cross the body's midline. Documented material supports balance and coordination work generally, but not this specific exercise (Ch 2).

## D
- **Default Mode Network (DMN)** — The brain's resting-state network active during daydreaming, mind-wandering, and self-reflection; in ADHD, it frequently defaults to self-critical rumination when left idling (Ch 1).
- **Delayed Sleep Phase Syndrome (DSPS)** — A circadian rhythm condition, common in ADHD, in which the internal clock is delayed by **two or more hours** `[verified]` — for some people this pushes the natural sleep window as late as roughly 3 a.m. to noon (Ch 9).
- **Dopamenu (Dopamine Menu)** `[practitioner-common]` — A structured menu of pre-decided healthy dopamine sources: Appetizers (quick boosts), Entrées (deep rejuvenation), Sides (task accompaniments), Desserts (temptations). A widely used ADHD-community tool, not tied to a single documented source (Ch 6).
- **Dopaminergic Tone** — The baseline concentration and receptor sensitivity of dopamine in the mesolimbic and prefrontal reward pathways (Ch 4, Ch 10).
- **Dysmetria** — Impaired cerebellar timing causing "overshooting" or "undershooting" in movement `[verified]`, and by extension in emotional reactions, time estimation, and task sequencing (Ch 2). The compound term "cognitive dysmetria" and the extension beyond movement are this skill's own inference, not a documented clinical term.
- **Dysbiosis** — An unhealthy imbalance in the gut microbiome where inflammatory bacteria suppress beneficial, neurotransmitter-producing microbes (Ch 10).

## E
- **Emotional Flooding** — A rapid, overwhelming surge of intense affect that temporarily disables prefrontal executive logic (Ch 8).
- **Emotional Regulation** — The executive ability to inhibit, modulate, and direct emotional responses in alignment with long-term goals (Ch 5, Ch 8).
- **Enteric Nervous System (ENS)** — The "second brain": **between 100 million and 500 million neurons** `[verified]`, more nerve cells than anywhere else in the body outside the brain itself, communicating bidirectionally with the brain via the vagus nerve (Ch 10).
- **Executive Functions** — The suite of top-down cognitive processes (inhibition, working memory, initiation, planning, cognitive flexibility, emotion regulation, self-monitoring) managed by prefrontal networks (Ch 5).
- **Externalizing the CEO** — Offloading executive tasks (memory, tracking, timing) from internal brain circuits onto visible physical and digital systems (Ch 5, Ch 7).

## F
- **Ferrari Engine with Bicycle Brakes** — A metaphor for high creative energy and processing speed paired with an underdeveloped inhibitory control system `[verified]` (Ch 1).
- **Food Colorings and Additives** — Meta-analyses (Nigg 2012, Pelsser 2017) find that eliminating food-coloring additives benefits some children with ADHD, estimating **10–30%** would likely respond. No specific dyes are named in documented sources (Ch 11).
- **Fidgeting** — An involuntary, adaptive micro-movement used by the nervous system to stimulate cortical arousal through cerebellar and vestibular feedback (Ch 2).
- **Friction Engineering (The 20-Second Rule)** `[practitioner-common]` — Decreasing physical friction for desired behaviors and increasing it for distractions. A widely used habit-design concept from outside this skill's documented sources (Ch 9).

## G
- **GABA (Gamma-Aminobutyric Acid)** — The brain's primary inhibitory neurotransmitter that acts as a natural neural brake to quiet anxiety and mental racing (Ch 12).
- **Gluten** — Wheat protein linked to ADHD via celiac disease and non-celiac gluten sensitivity; a gluten-free diet improved ADHD symptoms in celiac patients after six months (Niederhofer & Pittschieler, 2006). Sensitivity can present with neurologic symptoms and no digestive ones (Ch 10, Ch 11). The zonulin mechanism is not documented in this skill's sources.
- **Glycemic Load (GL)** — A metric assessing how significantly a food portion raises blood sugar; low-GL foods sustain steady focus without reactive hypoglycemia (Ch 11).

## H
- **Hyperfocus** — An intense, involuntary state of deep absorption in a dopamine-stimulating task, often accompanied by loss of time and environmental awareness (Ch 6).

## I
- **Inhibition** — The executive ability to pause and suppress an immediate impulse, distraction, or behavioral reaction (Ch 5).
- **Interest-Based Nervous System** — The neurobiological paradigm in which task engagement is governed by immediate interest, challenge, novelty, or urgency rather than abstract importance (Ch 6).
- **Iron** — Children with ADHD show lower iron and magnesium than controls; both are involved in dopamine synthesis `[verified]` (Ch 11).

## J
- **Junk Dopamine** `[practitioner-common]` — High-intensity, low-effort stimulation (algorithmic feeds, gambling-style games) that spikes dopamine but leaves prefrontal reserves depleted (Ch 6).

## L
- **Landing Zone / Launchpad** `[practitioner-common]` — A designated drop-station by the entrance or desk where daily essentials always live, preventing loss and frantic searching (Ch 7).
- **Leaky Gut (Intestinal Permeability)** — A compromised gut mucosal barrier that allows endotoxins into systemic circulation, provoking brain inflammation (Ch 10).
- **L-Theanine** — An amino acid found in green tea, documented as a calming adjunct `[verified]` (Ch 12). The "alpha brain wave" mechanism is `[practitioner-common]`, not documented in this skill's sources.

## M
- **Magnesium** — Children with ADHD show lower magnesium than controls; it is involved in dopamine synthesis `[verified]` (Ch 11, Ch 12). The specific *glycinate* form and its NMDA mechanism are `[practitioner-common]`, not documented in this skill's sources.
- **Masking** — The exhausting practice of concealing neurodivergent traits and overcompensating to meet neurotypical expectations, often leading to chronic burnout (Ch 8).
- **Microbiome** — The trillions of microorganisms living in the gut that manufacture neurotransmitters and regulate immune signaling (Ch 10).
- **Motivation Bridge** `[practitioner-common]` — Deliberately layering urgency, challenge, novelty, or interest onto a low-interest task to make starting possible (Ch 6).

## N
- **Now vs. Not Now** — The dual-state temporal model of ADHD where time is perceived either as the immediate moment ("NOW") or an undifferentiated void ("NOT NOW") (Ch 7).

## O
- **Object Permanence (for Intentions)** — The cognitive tendency for non-visible tasks, objects, or people to slip completely from conscious memory ("out of sight, out of existence") (Ch 7).
- **Omega-3 Fatty Acids (EPA & DHA)** — Essential polyunsaturated fatty acids central to nutritional-psychiatry findings on mood and the Mediterranean pattern. The "dopamine receptor density" mechanism is not documented in this skill's sources (Ch 11).

## P
- **PINCH Model** `[practitioner-common]` — Mnemonic for five motivation catalysts: **P**assion, **I**nterest, **N**ovelty, **C**ompetition, **H**urry. Not a documented term; the underlying documented framing is "urgency, challenge, novelty, and interest" `[verified]` (Ch 6).
- **Point of Performance** — The exact physical location and precise moment in time where an action needs to be executed; systems must be placed here to work (Ch 7).
- **Polyphenols** — Plant antioxidants that relieve the oxidative stress people with ADHD are at greater risk of; they act as low-dose compounds that train the body's stress response through **hormesis**, and influence neuron survival and regeneration `[verified]`. Richest documented sources: berries, cherries, eggplant, onions, kale, coffee, green tea (Ch 11).
- **Probiotics** — Live beneficial microorganisms in fermented foods (kimchi, kefir, sauerkraut) that support neurotransmitter synthesis (Ch 10).

## R
- **Rejection Sensitive Dysphoria (RSD)** — Severe emotional pain triggered by perceived rejection, criticism, or failure. Rejection sensitivity itself is well documented `[verified]`; the specific initialism "RSD" is `[practitioner-common]` (Ch 8).
- **Revenge Bedtime Procrastination** — Staying up late into the night to reclaim perceived autonomy and leisure after an exhausting day (Ch 9).
- **Right Difficult** — The concept of the optimal challenge-skill calibration that produces flow and sustained focus without boredom or panic `[verified]` (Ch 3).

## S
- **Short-Chain Fatty Acids (SCFAs)** — Anti-inflammatory bacterial fermentation byproducts (butyrate, acetate, propionate) that nourish brain health (Ch 10).
- **Sleep Inertia** `[practitioner-common]` — Grogginess and slow cognitive awakening on waking. A standard sleep-science term, but not tied to any single documented source here (Ch 9).
- **Stimulants (Methylphenidate / Amphetamines)** — First-line medications that inhibit dopamine and norepinephrine reuptake in prefrontal synapses (Ch 4).

## T
- **Task Initiation** — The executive capacity to cross the activation threshold and physically begin a task (Ch 5, Ch 6).
- **Task-Positive Network (TPN)** — The brain's goal-directed network that becomes active during external engagement, automatically silencing the rumination of the DMN (Ch 1).
- **Time Blindness** — The neurological inability to intuitively feel the passage of time or accurately estimate future task duration (Ch 7).
- **Transition Friction** — The acute mental and physical discomfort felt when shifting attention from one activity to another (Ch 6, Ch 9).
- **Tyrosine Hydroxylase** `[practitioner-common]` — A real biochemical enzyme, but not documented in this skill's sources; ch11's own precursor discussion uses phenylalanine and gut-bacterial synthesis instead. See [sources.md §1](sources.md#1-corrected-claims), correction 11.

## V
- **Vagus Nerve** — Gets its name from its wandering path from the brain stem down through the torso, branching extensively along the gut wall; carries signals in **both** directions between gut and brain `[verified]` (Ch 10).
- **VAST (Variable Attention Stimulus Trait)** — A non-stigmatizing framework covering both genetic ADHD and modern distraction-induced attentional fragmentation `[verified]` (Ch 1).
- **Vestibular System** — Sensory apparatus of the inner ear that tracks motion and gravity, feeding continuous balance signals into the cerebellum (Ch 2).
- **Visual Analog Timer** `[practitioner-common]` — A clock displaying remaining time as an evaporating colored disc, making time visible. Making time visible this way is a documented technique `[verified]`; specific commercial timer products are a community-sourced pointer, not a documented recommendation (Ch 7).
- **Vitamin Connect** — A term for warm human, animal, or community connection treated as a biological necessity rather than a nicety `[verified]` (Ch 3).

## W
- **Wall of Awful** `[practitioner-common]` — The emotional barrier built from past failures with a task — failure, disappointment, rejection, worry — which must be climbed *in addition to* doing the task itself. The more you've struggled with something before, the higher the wall. A term from the wider ADHD-coaching community, not this skill's own coinage (Ch 5).
- **Working Memory** — The limited mental workbench that holds and manipulates information in conscious awareness over short intervals (Ch 5, Ch 7).

## Z
- **Zinc** — Essential mineral cofactor that modulates dopamine transporter functioning and converts vitamin B6 into active P5P (Ch 11).
- **Zing Performance** `[verified]` — A commercial vestibular-training program used for a range of issues from spatial awareness to learning difficulties. The subject is first assessed on eye-tracking speed, accuracy, and attention span, then given a series of exercises (Ch 2).
