# Chapter 16: Rejection Sensitivity in Self-Worth and Relationships

**Domain**: ADHD emotional regulation — RSD with yourself, partners, friends, and family

*Content in this file draws on published clinical and educational material on ADHD, paraphrased in this skill's own words and tagged `[verified]` at claims traceable to that material. Untagged claims are this skill's own synthesis; `[practitioner-common]` marks community-sourced tools.*

## Core Idea
RSD does most of its damage in close relationships, where the stakes feel highest and the signals are most ambiguous. Recurring situations — the slow reply, the stinging joke, the family critique — become manageable by sorting each one into urgent, lingering, or between-people and reaching for that group of the regulation kit ([ch15](ch15-regulation-kit-twelve-tools.md)). The shift is from reacting to the story to responding to the facts.

---

## Frameworks Introduced

- **Situation → First Move** (this skill's routing, via [Pattern 7](../patterns.md#pattern-7-the-emotion-type-tool-selector)):
  - *Formulation*: Classify the moment first — **urgent** (in the body now), **lingering** (in the head for hours or days), or **between people** (something needs saying) — then pick from that group of the regulation kit ([ch15](ch15-regulation-kit-twelve-tools.md)). Relational moves only after the body has settled.

  | Situation | Usually starts as | First move |
  |---|---|---|
  | Unanswered text / gap between messages | Urgent | Ground the body, then write the facts apart from the story ([ch08](ch08-emotions-rsd-relationships.md)) |
  | Urge to keep asking "are we okay?" | Urgent | Delay — let the urge crest before deciding whether to ask once |
  | Replaying a conversation | Lingering | Emotion Labeling, then Thought Check |
  | A joke that stung | Between people | Once calm, ask a plain clarifying question rather than assuming intent |
  | Regret after oversharing | Lingering | Self-Compassion; others usually notice far less than it feels (the spotlight effect) |
  | Cancelled plans | Urgent, then lingering | Breathing or movement first; later, Resilient Reframe |
  | Brushing off compliments | Lingering | Neutral Observation of the urge to deflect; answer with a simple thanks |
  | Comparison or jealousy | Lingering | Thought Check on the comparison itself |
  | Family criticism | Between people | Ground first, then a boundary or a request to revisit it later |
  | Carrying everyone's feelings | Between people | Boundaries |
  | Urge to end it before being left | Urgent | Delay — no relationship decisions until the surge has passed, ideally after sleeping on it |

- **The Apology Decision Rule** `[verified]`:
  - *Formulation*: If you believe you did something wrong, apologize sincerely. If you're only afraid the other person is upset, the apology is managing your anxiety — check in, clarify, or set a boundary instead.
  - *How to apply*: Swap reflexive apologies for thanks: "sorry for talking so much" → "thanks for listening."

- **After-Action Check**:
  - *Formulation*: Once the moment has passed, reuse ch08's fact-vs-story split: what literally happened, what story was added, and one thing to keep or change next time. Repetition matters more than getting the answers right; the new reading usually feels unconvincing at first.

## Key Concepts

- **Reassurance Loop** `[verified]`: Repeatedly asking for reassurance soothes briefly, then builds dependence. Self-regulate first, then connect.
- **Timeline vs. Self-Worth Comparison** `[verified]`: Comparing *when* life milestones happen is different from comparing *who you are*; the second is the one that wounds.
- **Secondary Shame**: A DBT-style secondary emotion — shame about having reacted at all, layered on the original hurt. Treat it with self-compassion, not more criticism.
- **Reactive Hyperindependence** `[verified]`: Extreme self-reliance learned after past hurt — refusing help to avoid disappointment.
- **Ending Is Allowed** `[verified]`: When boundaries have repeatedly failed, ending or distancing from a relationship that keeps dysregulating you is legitimate.
- **Pre-Agreed Exit Cue** `[verified]`: A signal arranged with a partner before a hard gathering, so stepping out takes no explanation. A one-to-two minute break, or clenching and releasing fists under the table, also works.
- **Default Phrase** `[verified]`: "I'll mull that over and let you know" — a ready-made delay for intrusive questions and pressure.
- **"No" Without a Speech** `[verified]`: Decline briefly; over-explaining invites negotiation.

## Mental Models

- **Pattern, Not Verdict**: One slow reply is data. A repeated pattern — always doing all the emotional work — is worth naming with an I-Statement.
- **Regulate, Then Relate**: Connection after grounding repairs; connection during a surge often escalates.

---

## Anti-patterns

- **Sending the balanced text mid-storm**: *Why it fails*: A calm, fair message is nearly impossible while flooded. Put the phone out of reach.
- **Reflexive over-apologizing**: *Why it fails*: It trains others to see you as at fault and keeps your worth contingent on their mood.
- **Overcompensating to stay chosen**: Doing the most so you won't be forgotten. *Why it fails*: It drains you and stops the relationship from showing whether it holds without the extra effort.

## Worked Example (Study Depth)

### Case: The Good-Night Text That Didn't Come

- **Trigger**: A partner who always texts good night doesn't, one evening.
- **Brain read**: "They're pulling away." Chest tightness; urge to send three messages.
- **Tool**: Mindful Breathing (4-2-6 × 5), then Delay with the phone in another room until the urge to send passes.
- **Thought Check**: Evidence for pulling away? None besides tonight. Evidence against? Dinner plans tomorrow; they mentioned being exhausted.
- **Next day, if still raw**: I-Statement — "I felt unsettled when the good-night text didn't come; a quick 'heading to bed' would help me."
- **Outcome**: They'd fallen asleep early. One calm message replaced a spiral of six.

---

## Key Takeaways

1. Sort the moment — urgent, lingering, or between people — before choosing a tool.
2. Ground before reaching out; reassurance-seeking builds a loop.
3. Apologize for wrongs, not for existing.
4. Boundaries govern your behavior; ending a relationship that won't respect them is allowed.
