# Chapter 11: Evidence-Based ADHD Nutritional Protocols and Elimination Strategies

**Domain**: ADHD nutrition — dietary protocols and elimination strategies (the primary nutrition-focused chapter in this skill's sources)

*Content in this file draws on published clinical and educational material on nutritional psychiatry, paraphrased in this skill's own words and tagged `[verified]` at claims traceable to that material. Untagged claims are this skill's own synthesis; `[practitioner-common]` marks community-sourced tools.*

> **Not medical advice.** This chapter summarizes documented nutritional-psychiatry findings. Elimination diets, supplement dosing, and anything touching stimulant medication belong with a doctor.

## Core Idea
Diet is an **adjunct** to ADHD treatment, not a replacement for it — managing ADHD well takes both appropriate medical treatment and appropriate nutrition, working together `[verified]`. The evidence supports an overall Mediterranean eating pattern, attention to gluten and A1 milk caseins in sensitive individuals, polyphenols against oxidative stress, and adequate zinc, iron, and magnesium. It does **not** support sugar as a cause of ADHD.

## Frameworks Introduced

- **Dietary Micronutrients** `[verified]`:
  - *Formulation*: What documented research actually supports, without added mechanism:
    - **Zinc**: animal and human studies indicate hyperactivity may occur where zinc is deficient; zinc deficiencies are associated with ADHD in children, in part because low zinc dampens dopamine-dependent reward-pathway activity.
    - **Iron and Magnesium**: children with ADHD show lower levels of iron and magnesium than controls on average, and both minerals are involved in dopamine synthesis.
    - **Vitamin C, vitamin B1, potassium, and nuts**: a 2017 study of 318 children found consumption of these improved performance on a test of information-processing speed. More mushrooms tracked with better reasoning; noodles and fast food lowered scores.
  - *When to use*: Designing daily meals and discussing supplementation with a medical provider.
  - *Note*: documented sources give **no dosages** and do not name ferritin specifically. They do not claim omega-3s raise dopamine receptor density.

- **Foods That Worsen ADHD** `[verified]`:
  - *Formulation*: Three factors stand out, in descending order of evidence:
    1. **Gluten**: a well-established link via celiac disease. Niederhofer & Pittschieler (2006) found people with celiac disease were more likely to have ADHD, and that a gluten-free diet improved symptoms after six months. **Non-celiac gluten sensitivity** is less conclusive but plausible — and it can present with neurologic symptoms and *no* digestive ones, which is why it gets missed.
    2. **Dairy — specifically A1 beta-casein**: Sun Jianqin (2016) had 45 participants drink A1+A2 milk and then A2-only milk. On A1, subjects had more gastrointestinal inflammation, slower thinking, and more errors on an information-processing test. Prefer A2, nut, goat's, or sheep's milk.
    3. **Food colorings and additives**: traced to Feingold's 1976 elimination diet and the later **few foods diet**. A 1983 meta-analysis found the Feingold effect weak. But Nigg (2012) and Pelsser (2017) found eliminating food-coloring additives benefited some children, estimating **10–30% would likely respond**. Worth trying only if symptoms don't respond to gentler measures.
  - **On sugar**: the evidence review concludes **sugar does not cause ADHD** — Del-Ponte (2019) found no consistent association. Limiting it is still worthwhile for general health, but not as an ADHD-specific intervention.
  - *When to use*: An elimination trial, ideally with clinical supervision — remove one food at a time, record symptom changes, then reintroduce one at a time (the method Albert Rowe pioneered in 1926).

- **The "Focus Plate" Blueprint**:
  - *Formulation*: Every meal should combine three elements to ensure steady dopamine synthesis and stable blood sugar:
    - **Clean Bioavailable Protein**: Wild salmon, organic poultry, pasture-raised eggs, pumpkin seeds, hemp hearts.
    - **Complex Fiber & Polyphenols**: Dark leafy greens, broccoli, blueberries, blackberries, asparagus.
    - **Healthy Neuro-Protective Fats**: Extra virgin olive oil, avocados, walnuts, chia seeds.

## Key Concepts

- **Polyphenols** `[verified]`: Verlaet (2018) found dietary polyphenols useful against ADHD by relieving oxidative stress, which people with ADHD are at greater risk of. They act as low-dose plant compounds that condition the body's stress-response machinery through **hormesis**, and influence neuron survival and regeneration. **Richest documented sources: berries, cherries, eggplant, onions, kale, coffee, and green tea.**
- **Glycemic Load (GL)**: A measure of how much a specific portion of carbohydrate raises blood glucose; low-GL foods sustain steady focus for hours.
- **Phenylalanine and precursor synthesis** `[verified]`: Dopamine and noradrenaline cannot cross the blood-brain barrier, but their precursors can — and gut bacteria make them. Aarts (2017) found people with ADHD had more phenylalanine-producing bacteria, and the less their brains responded to reward, the more of those bacteria were present. The researchers' own caution: the sure conclusion is only that gut disturbances correlate with brain disturbances, not a specific causal mechanism.
- **Microbial Fermentation Byproducts**: Beneficial short-chain fatty acids produced from eating diverse colored vegetables.

### The ADHD Nutrition Quick-Guide

*The single strongest overall recommendation across this skill's sources is the **Mediterranean eating pattern**: Ríos-Hernández (2017) studied 120 children and adolescents and found those who did not adhere to it were more likely to have ADHD. The table below is a practical reading of that pattern; the "Eliminate" column is narrower than an earlier version of this file claimed.*

| Category | Include Daily (Focus Boosters) | Eliminate / Minimize (Focus Crashers) |
|---|---|---|
| **Proteins** | Wild salmon, pasture-raised eggs, organic poultry, lentils, hemp hearts | Processed lunch meats, conventionally factory-farmed meats with additives |
| **Fats** | Extra virgin olive oil, avocado, walnuts, chia seeds, flaxseed | Hydrogenated trans-fats, industrial seed oils (corn, soy, cheap canola) |
| **Carbs & Fiber** | Berries, sweet potatoes, oats, quinoa, leafy greens, asparagus | Sugary cereals, pastries, white bread, high-fructose corn syrup |
| **Beverages** | Water with lemon, matcha, green tea, rooibos tea, bone broth | Sugary sodas, commercial fruit juices, neon-colored energy drinks |
| **Additives** | Turmeric with black pepper, rosemary, oregano | Food colorings and additives generally — no specific dyes are named in documented sources |
| **Caffeine** | Tea and coffee, **under 400 mg/day** for adults; none for children | Above 400 mg/day — causes overexcitability |

## Anti-patterns

- **Skipping Breakfast or Eating Only Refined Carbs**: Having a bagel, donut, or sugary coffee drink on an empty stomach. *Why it fails*: Creates an immediate insulin surge followed by a crash, leaving the prefrontal cortex starved of steady glucose by 10:00 AM.
- **Over-Supplementing Without Bloodwork**: Taking megadoses of isolated minerals without testing baseline levels (especially iron and zinc). *Why it fails*: Excess minerals can cause toxicity or induce deficiencies in competing minerals. Documented sources give no dosages — treat supplementation as a medical decision.
- **Treating Diet as a Replacement for Treatment**: Pursuing elimination diets instead of medication or therapy. *Why it fails*: Diet works *alongside* other treatments, not instead of them — ADHD `[verified]` frequently resists treatment enough that every workable lever, including nutrition, is worth adding rather than substituting.

## Worked Example (Study Depth)

### Case: Implementing an ADHD-Friendly Weekly Meal Prep Protocol

- **Goal**: Eliminate morning decision fatigue and prevent impulsive junk-food choices during midday focus hours.
- **Batch-Prep Strategy**:
  1. *Breakfast Jars*: 5 mason jars pre-filled with rolled oats, chia seeds, pumpkin seeds (rich in Zinc and Magnesium), Ceylon cinnamon, and frozen blueberries. (Add unsweetened almond milk the night before).
  2. *Brain-Protective Lunch Prep*: Batch-roast 4 chicken breasts and 2 trays of seasoned vegetables (broccoli, bell peppers, purple cabbage). Portion into glass containers with quinoa and extra virgin olive oil.
  3. *Emergency Desk Snacks*: Raw walnuts, dry-roasted pumpkin seeds, 85% dark chocolate squares, and canned wild sardines in olive oil (high EPA/DHA).
- **Result**: Zero mid-morning glucose crashes; executive functioning remains stable through 5:00 PM.

## Key Takeaways

1. **Diet is an adjunct, not a substitute.** Managing ADHD well takes both appropriate medical treatment and appropriate nutrition, together.
2. The **Mediterranean eating pattern** has the strongest overall support of anything in this chapter.
3. **Zinc, iron, and magnesium** are the micronutrients documented sources tie to ADHD; low levels are associated with symptoms. No dosages are given.
4. **Polyphenols** — berries, cherries, eggplant, onions, kale, coffee, green tea — counter the oxidative stress ADHD brains are more prone to.
5. **Gluten and A1 caseins** are worth investigating in sensitive individuals; gluten sensitivity can present with no digestive symptoms at all.
6. **Sugar does not cause ADHD** — a popular belief the evidence explicitly rejects. Limit it for general health instead.
7. **Food-coloring elimination** helps an estimated 10–30%, and is a later resort, not a first move.
