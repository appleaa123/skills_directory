# Chapter 9: Sleep Architecture, Energy Rhythms, and Friction Engineering

**Domain**: ADHD sleep and habit formation — circadian rhythms and friction engineering

*Content in this file draws on published clinical and educational material on ADHD, paraphrased in this skill's own words and tagged `[verified]` at claims traceable to that material. Untagged claims are this skill's own synthesis; `[practitioner-common]` marks community-sourced tools.*

## Core Idea
Sleep disturbances in ADHD—especially Delayed Sleep Phase Syndrome and bedtime procrastination—are biological circadian realities driven by delayed melatonin release and hyperactive evening thought loops; mastering sleep and energy requires designing low-friction wind-down rituals and aligning daily demands with personal neurodivergent energy rhythms.

---

## Frameworks Introduced

- **Delayed Sleep Phase Architecture (The DSPS Reality)**:
  - *Formulation*: Delayed sleep phase syndrome occurs when the internal clock is delayed by **two or more hours** `[verified]` — for some people this pushes the natural sleep window as late as roughly 3 a.m. to noon. ADHDers tend toward a later chronotype, meaning melatonin is released later. Forcing a standard "early to bed, early to rise" schedule often means hours of frustrating wakefulness. *(An earlier version claimed "up to 75%" and "1.5 to 2 hours"; neither figure is documented.)*
  - *When to use*: Designing sustainable work schedules and sleep-wake patterns.
  - *How to apply*: Align your core commitments with your natural chronotype when possible. When earlier waking is required, shift circadian cues systematically using morning bright light (lux exposure), physical movement, and strict evening blue-light elimination.

- **The Evening Friction Runway (Anti-Bedtime Procrastination)**:
  - *Formulation*: Revenge bedtime procrastination is rarely about wanting to stay awake; it is about avoiding the high executive friction of the bedtime transition (brushing teeth, washing face, shutting down screens, changing clothes).
  - *When to use*: Ending the midnight doom-scrolling loop.
  - *How to apply*: Complete all physical bedtime preparations (brushing teeth, getting into pajamas, plugging phone across the room) *early in the evening* (e.g. 8:00 PM), so going to sleep when tired requires zero executive steps.

- **Friction Engineering (The 20-Second Rule)** `[practitioner-common]` — a widely used habit-design concept from outside this skill's documented sources:
  - *Formulation*: The probability of an ADHD brain initiating a behavior is inversely proportional to the physical and mental friction required to start:
    - **Decrease friction for good habits**: Leave workout clothes on top of shoes; keep water bottle filled on desk; leave book open on pillow.
    - **Increase friction for distractions**: Log out of social apps; put video game controllers in a high closet; use physical lock boxes.
  - *When to use*: Building daily routines that stick without willpower.

## Key Concepts

- **Revenge Bedtime Procrastination**: Staying up late to reclaim personal autonomy and leisure time after a day of masking and executive exhaustion.
- **Sleep Inertia** `[practitioner-common]`: A real sleep-science term for grogginess and disorientation on waking, but it appears **zero** times in any of the three books, and the "30–60 minutes" duration is not from the source — see [sources.md §2](../sources.md#2-practitioner-common--real-but-not-from-these-books).
- **Habit Stacking onto Sensory Anchors**: Attaching a new habit to an existing, inevitable physical action (e.g. *"While the electric kettle boils, I take my morning supplements"*).
- **The Dopamine Wind-Down Trap**: Consuming high-intensity entertainment right before bed, which over-stimulates the brain and blocks sleep onset.

---

## Mental Models

- **Sleep is the Master Executive Fuel**: Lost sleep degrades executive function the next day and amplifies ADHD symptoms. More severe ADHD symptoms track with more severe sleep-disorder symptoms, and vice versa `[verified]`. *(An earlier version put precise numbers on this — "half" and "200%" — that appear in no documented source.)*
- **Make the Right Choice the Lazy Choice**: Structure your environment so that doing what you intended requires the least effort.

---

## Anti-patterns

- **Bringing the Phone into Bed**: Using the smartphone as an alarm clock on the nightstand. *Why it fails*: An open internet browser is an infinite dopamine machine waiting to hijack your attention during low-inhibition moments.
- **Trying to Form Habits Through Repetition Alone**: Believing that doing something for "21 days" will make it automatic. *Why it fails*: ADHD brains do not automate habits easily due to dopamine signaling deficits; habits require permanent environmental scaffolding.

## Worked Example (Study Depth)

### Case: Fixing the 2:00 AM Bedtime Procrastination Loop

- **Problem**: Individual intends to sleep at 11:00 PM, but at 10:45 PM feels exhausted by the idea of getting up to brush teeth and wash face, so stays on the couch scrolling until 2:30 AM.
- **Redesigned Evening Protocol**:
  1. *08:30 PM — Pre-Emptive Night Routine*: Immediately after dinner, brush teeth, wash face, put on pajamas, set morning clothes out. (Friction eliminated while energy is still present).
  2. *09:30 PM — Charging Dock Out of Reach*: Phone is plugged into a charging dock in the kitchen.
  3. *10:00 PM — Low-Dopamine Wind-Down*: Read physical fiction book or listen to an audiobook with dim amber lighting.
  4. *10:45 PM — Frictionless Slide*: When eyes feel heavy, simply slide under the covers and turn off bedside lamp.
- **Result**: Sleep onset occurs naturally by 11:15 PM; morning grogginess drops drastically.

## Key Takeaways

1. ADHD sleep struggles are biologically driven by delayed circadian rhythms and high transition friction.
2. Complete your bedtime physical routine early in the evening before executive battery reaches 0%.
3. Habit formation in ADHD relies on permanent friction reduction and visual cues, not automatic muscle memory.
4. Keep the phone outside the bedroom; replace high-dopamine feeds with soothing audio or physical reading.
