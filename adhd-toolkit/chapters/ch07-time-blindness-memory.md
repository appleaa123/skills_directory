# Chapter 7: Seeing Time, Point-of-Performance Design, and Memory Externalization

**Domain**: ADHD executive function — time perception and memory externalization

*Content in this file draws on published clinical and educational material on ADHD, paraphrased in this skill's own words and tagged `[verified]` at claims traceable to that material. Untagged claims are this skill's own synthesis; `[practitioner-common]` marks community-sourced tools.*

## Core Idea
The ADHD brain experiences time in two fundamental states—**"NOW"** and **"NOT NOW"**—and operates with a severely constrained working memory scratchpad; surviving and thriving requires transforming abstract time and internal memory into vivid, external physical representations at the exact point of performance.

---

## Frameworks Introduced

- **The "Now vs. Not Now" Time Architecture**:
  - *Formulation*: The internal temporal clock in ADHD does not sense time as a continuous, linear stream. Events are either occurring in the immediate present ("NOW") or banished to an undifferentiated future ("NOT NOW"). As a consequence, deadlines remain invisible until they abruptly crash into the "NOW" zone.
  - *When to use*: Estimating project durations, preventing chronic tardiness, and managing deadlines.
  - *How to apply*: Convert "Not Now" events into visible visual representations by using analog clocks, colored calendar blocks, and backward scheduling.

- **Point-of-Performance Design**:
  - *Formulation*: Information, reminders, and tools must be placed at the exact physical space and precise moment where the action must take place. Internal memory will fail; the environment must trigger the behavior automatically.
  - *When to use*: Organizing workspaces, remembering keys/medications/supplements, submitting recurring forms.
  - *How to apply*: Put vitamins directly next to the coffee maker; hang car keys on a hook directly beside the doorknob; tape the daily checklist onto the laptop monitor.

- **Landing Zones & Launchpads** `[practitioner-common]`:
  - *Formulation*: A dedicated, friction-free drop zone by the front door or desk where all portable essentials (keys, wallet, badge, phone, bag) reside exclusively.
  - *When to use*: Eliminating the morning frantic search panic.
  - *How to apply*: Create a simple, open bowl or shelf. Establish one non-negotiable rule: the moment you enter the room, essentials are dropped into the launchpad before anything else.

- **The "ADHD Tax" Buffer Formula**:
  - *Formulation*: Time estimates made by an ADHD brain represent ideal execution without friction, transitions, or distractions.
  - *When to use*: Setting schedules and scheduling travel or project delivery dates.
  - *How to apply*: Take your intuitive time estimate and apply the **1.5x + 15 Minute Rule** (e.g. A task estimated at 30 minutes gets scheduled as `30 * 1.5 + 15 = 60 minutes`).

## Key Concepts

- **Time Blindness**: An inability to intuitively track the passage of time or accurately gauge how long a future task will take.
- **Working Memory Bottleneck**: The rapid decay of information held in mind when interrupted by even minor sensory inputs.
- **Visual Analog Timers** `[practitioner-common]`: Timers showing time as an evaporating colored disc, translating abstract numbers into visible space. Making time visible this way is a documented technique `[verified]`; specific commercial timer products are a community-sourced pointer, not a documented recommendation.
- **Object Permanence for Intentions**: The tendency for non-visible tasks, objects, or ideas to be completely forgotten ("out of sight, out of existence").
- **Backward Planning (Reverse Scheduling)**: Planning from the hard deadline backward step-by-step to calculate the actual required start time, including transit, setup, and buffer times.

---

## Mental Models

- **Digital Clocks are Numbers; Analog Clocks are Shapes**: A digital clock only tells you what time it is; an analog clock shows you how much time is left.
- **Your Brain is a Processor, Not a Hard Drive**: Free up RAM by dumping every task, idea, and reminder into an external capture tool immediately.

---

## Anti-patterns

- **Relying on "I'll Remember This Later"**: Storing commitments, appointments, or bright ideas in internal memory without immediate capture. *Why it fails*: Working memory clears within seconds when new stimuli arrive.
- **Using Opaque Storage Boxes**: Putting items you need to use inside closed drawers, solid bins, or nested folders. *Why it fails*: If you cannot see it, your brain treats it as if it no longer exists.

## Worked Example (Study Depth)

### Case: Preventing Chronic Meeting Tardiness with Backward Planning

- **Target**: Arrive at an in-person client meeting at 10:00 AM.
- **Typical Flawed Estimation**: *"The office is 20 minutes away, so I'll leave at 9:40 AM."* (Results in leaving at 9:50 AM, hitting traffic, arriving late at 10:15 AM stressed).
- **Reverse Scheduling Matrix**:
  | Step | Activity | Duration | Scheduled Time |
  |---|---|---|---|
  | 7 | Walk into meeting room | — | 10:00 AM |
  | 6 | Park car, walk from garage, take elevator | 10 min | 09:50 AM |
  | 5 | Drive to office (with traffic buffer) | 30 min | 09:20 AM |
  | 4 | "ADHD Tax" transition buffer (shoes, keys, last check) | 15 min | 09:05 AM |
  | 3 | Pack laptop bag from Launchpad | 5 min | 09:00 AM |
  | 2 | Get dressed & final prep | 30 min | 08:30 AM |
  | 1 | Wake up, breakfast, medication | 45 min | 07:45 AM |
- **True Departure Time**: 09:05 AM (instead of 9:40 AM).
- **Result**: Arrives at 09:50 AM with zero stress and full composure.

---

## Key Takeaways

1. Time blindness is a neurodevelopmental trait; make time visible using analog shapes and disappearing disks.
2. Working memory is fragile; externalize every task, thought, and deadline to the point of performance.
3. Use transparent bins, open shelves, and visual hooks because "out of sight is out of existence."
4. Always calculate the ADHD Tax by multiplying initial time estimates by 1.5 and adding transition buffers.
