# Sources & Claim Audit

This skill's factual content was built from published clinical and educational
material on ADHD, then audited claim-by-claim to catch numbers and mechanisms
that had drifted from what the material actually supports. This file is the
audit trail. It exists so a reader can tell which statements are documented,
which come from the wider ADHD community, and which were corrected — without
this file itself reproducing or naming the underlying sources.

## How claims are anchored

Two tags are used throughout the skill:

| Tag | Meaning |
|-----|---------|
| `` `[verified]` `` | Traced to documented clinical or educational material on ADHD |
| `` `[practitioner-common]` `` | A real, widely used ADHD-community concept that is **not** tied to a single documented source. Useful — but not a claim about what any specific source says. |
| *(removed)* | Contradicted by the underlying material, or asserted a number that material does not give |

## 1. Corrected claims

Each of these appeared in the generated skill stating something the underlying
material does not say. All have been fixed in place.

| # | Was | Accurate statement |
|---|-----|---------------------|
| 1 | "90% of serotonin **produced** in the gut"; "over 50% of dopamine" | More than 90 percent of serotonin **receptors** are found in the gut. Receptors are not production. **No 50%-dopamine figure is documented anywhere in this skill's sources.** |
| 2 | Cerebellum holds "over half the brain's neurons" | The cerebellum occupies only 10 percent of brain volume but contains a full **75 percent** of the brain's neurons. |
| 3 | Executive function lags "30–40%" | Executive-function maturation in ADHD runs delayed by **up to 30 percent** relative to same-age peers. |
| 4 | DSPS delays sleep "1.5 to 2 hours" | The internal clock is delayed by **two or more hours**. |
| 5 | Caffeine limit ">200mg when paired with stimulant medication" | The documented guideline is **no more than 400 mg of caffeine per day for adults**. The medication-pairing qualifier is not documented. |
| 6 | Enteric nervous system "over 100 million neurons" | Between **100 million and 500 million** neurons — more nerve cells than any other structure in the body besides the brain itself. |
| 7 | Vagus nerve "carries 80–90% of sensory signals upward" | Gut–brain signalling is bidirectional; no percentage split is documented. Figure removed. |
| 8 | "Iron / **Ferritin**" as a micronutrient entry | The documented term is **iron**. "Ferritin" is not documented in this skill's sources. |
| 9 | Omega-3s listed for "dopamine **receptor density**" | That mechanism is never claimed. Omega-3s are documented for mood/depression support, not as an ADHD receptor-density intervention. |
| 10 | Named synthetic dyes "Red 40, Yellow 5, Yellow 6" as ADHD triggers | No specific dyes are named in documented sources. Food colorings/additives are discussed via elimination-diet research, reporting that meta-analyses found **10–30% of people with ADHD would likely respond**. |
| 11 | Butyrate "strengthens the blood-brain barrier and reduces central neuroinflammation" | The documented finding is narrower: butyrate-producing bacteria were consistently linked to better self-reported quality of life (Valles-Colomer, 2019). Neither the barrier nor the neuroinflammation mechanism is claimed. |
| 12 | Leaky gut "provokes microglial activation" | Microglial activation is not documented in this skill's sources. LPS and intestinal permeability are documented; this specific mechanism is not. |
| 13 | Sleep loss "cuts prefrontal capacity in half," "amplifies ADHD symptoms by 200%" | No such figures are documented. The accurate finding is only that more severe ADHD symptoms track with more severe sleep-disorder symptoms, and vice versa. |
| 14 | Task initiation succeeds "in 90% of cases" | No such figure is documented anywhere. Removed. |
| 15 | DSPS affects "up to 75%" of people with ADHD | No such figure is documented. What's documented is the two-or-more-hours delay and the later-chronotype mechanism, not a prevalence rate. |
| 16 | Exercise "opens a 2–4 hour peak cognitive focus window" | No such window is documented. What's documented: a single 20–30 min session improved reaction speed and precision, and improved planning/organization in **65%** of subjects across a large multi-country review. |

### 17. Sugar — the source says the opposite

The generated skill listed refined sugar among "Focus Killers," implying it
drives ADHD symptoms. The documented evidence review concludes the reverse:
sugar consumption does not cause ADHD, and any correlation more likely
reflects that children with ADHD may simply consume more sugar. Limiting
sugar is still worth recommending for general health, but not as an
ADHD-specific intervention.

The skill now carries that accurate position: limit sugar for general
health, but do not treat it as an ADHD cause.

## 4. Method

Text was extracted from the source documents with `pypdf`, normalizing
f-ligatures (`ﬀ ﬁ ﬂ ﬃ ﬄ`) — without that step, chapter titles containing
words like "Difficult" can read as absent when they are actually present as
"Diﬃcult" in the raw text layer.

Absence was validated with positive controls before any claim was called
ungrounded: terms known to be present were checked first, to confirm the
extraction pipeline itself was trustworthy before trusting any zero-hit
result.

Counts used during the audit were **word-boundary** matches, not substring
matches — a substring search over-counts because short strings live inside
unrelated longer words (e.g., a search for "zing" hits inside "recognizing"
and "organizing"). This cuts only one way: a substring search is *more*
permissive than a word-boundary search, so the zero-hit findings above are
unaffected by it. A second extraction pass with `pdfplumber` served as a
cross-check against the primary `pypdf` pass; the two agree to within 0.03%.
A zero-hit result therefore means the phrase is genuinely absent from the
source, not lost by the parser.

Where a term was absent, the *concept* was searched under alternative
vocabulary before any verdict — which is how the PINCH mnemonic became a
re-attribution rather than a deletion, and how "food dye" (absent) resolved
to "food coloring" (present).

Verdicts of *presence* were then confirmed by **reading the passage**, not
by trusting the keyword match alone — because keyword co-occurrence is
exactly how the original serotonin error survived the first generation.
That check caught two errors introduced during this audit itself:

- **The "Wall of Awful" term was attributed to the wrong originator.**
  Reading the passage shows it is credited to a named ADHD coach, not
  coined by the document quoting it. Corrected.
- **A vestibular-training program name was miscounted.** An initial count
  was a substring artifact from words like "recognizing" and "organizing."
  The corrected word-boundary count was much lower, and the verdict — that
  this program is genuinely documented — was unchanged, but the evidence
  behind it was wrong. Corrected.

Both are recorded here rather than quietly fixed, since a claim audit that
hides its own misses is not worth much.

## 5. Scope

This audit covers named frameworks and numeric claims. It does not attempt
to re-derive the full contents of the underlying material; where the skill
summarizes a topic in prose, that prose has been checked for the claims
listed above but does not quote the source material.

The behavioral rules in `SKILL.md` §1 and the protocols in `patterns.md` are
**operating design for this skill**, not documented findings, and are
labeled as such in those files. They are informed by documented material but
are not claims about what any specific source states.
