# Workflow: Energy Crash

**Route here from**: DEPLETED — crashed, foggy, "I can't think straight," no fuel.

> **Not medical advice.** This workflow summarizes documented nutritional-psychiatry findings on ADHD. It is not clinical guidance, does not prescribe dosages, and does not replace a doctor — especially for anything touching stimulant medication or an actual eating disorder.

## The failure mode this avoids

The default response to "I can't focus" is a focus technique — Pomodoro, time-blocking, a productivity tip — offered before checking whether the brain in question has eaten anything. A depleted body cannot execute a technique; the technique isn't the bottleneck. **Body first, always** — this is the DEPLETED routing rule from SKILL.md §0, and this workflow is what "body first" actually means in practice.

## Procedure

1. **Ask what and when they last ate, and their caffeine intake today** — three short facts, not an interview. This determines almost everything that follows.

2. **If it's been more than ~4 hours since real food, or the day started on caffeine alone**: that's very likely the whole problem. Say so plainly, then suggest something from the documented list — **do not improvise beyond it**:
   - Protein: eggs, salmon, poultry, hemp or pumpkin seed protein.
   - Something with the documented polyphenols: berries, cherries, eggplant, onions, kale, coffee, green tea.
   - Water — mild dehydration alone measurably reduces working memory and attention.
   (Full grounding: [ch10](../chapters/ch10-gut-brain-axis.md)–[ch12](../chapters/ch12-anxiety-insomnia-adjuncts.md), [cheatsheet.md §5](../cheatsheet.md), [patterns.md#pattern-6](../patterns.md#pattern-6-the-adhd-focus-plate--micronutrient-stacking-protocol).)

3. **If caffeine is already at or above 400 mg for the day** — the documented adult ceiling — name that directly as a likely contributor to the crash, not just a thing to avoid next time. Overexcitability from excess caffeine can produce the same foggy, can't-focus feeling it's meant to fix.

4. **Never invent a dosage, a supplement amount, or a food not in the documented list.** If asked something not covered — "how much magnesium should I take?" — say plainly that no documented dosage exists and this is a question for a doctor or pharmacist. Making up a plausible-sounding number here is the single most dangerous failure this skill can produce.

5. **Never suggest cutting sugar as the fix.** The documented evidence review concludes that sugar does not cause ADHD — see [sources.md §17](../sources.md#17-sugar--the-source-says-the-opposite). If sugar comes up, the accurate framing is "worth moderating for general health," never "this is why you're crashing."

6. **After the immediate suggestion, ask if it's actually food, or if it's sleep debt or a missed dose** — a crash has more than one possible cause, and this workflow only owns the nutrition branch. If the answer points to sleep or medication, that's a different conversation — say so and hand it off rather than stretching this file's authority to cover it.

## Ends in

One concrete thing to eat or drink right now, drawn from the documented list, plus a one-line note on whether the pattern (timing, caffeine load) is worth a longer look later — not a full nutrition plan, and never a supplement dosage.
