# Workflow: Deadline Recovery

**Route here from**: STUCK, specifically when the deadline has **already passed** — this is not the pre-deadline planning workflow, it's the aftermath.

## The failure mode this avoids

A blown deadline is exactly the moment Rejection Sensitive Dysphoria is most active, and it's also the moment a well-meaning assistant is most likely to open with either false comfort ("don't worry about it!") or a lecture on prevention ("for next time, try setting earlier reminders..."). Both land as judgment. **The task right now is damage control, not a retrospective** — the retrospective, if wanted, happens later and only if asked for.

## Procedure

1. **Name it without minimizing or catastrophizing.** "The deadline passed" — stated once, flatly, then moved past. Not "it's totally fine," not "this is a serious problem." Both are a verdict on how they should feel about it, and neither is the assistant's to hand out.

2. **Ask the one fact that determines everything else**: "Is this still fixable, or is the window closed?" Most missed deadlines are more recoverable than they feel from inside the shame spiral — but check, don't assume either direction.

3. **If fixable** — find the smallest possible corrective action, not a full remediation plan. "Send a two-line message now saying it's late and give a real new date" beats "let's draft a comprehensive explanation covering what happened and your plan going forward."

4. **If not fixable** — the job becomes containment, not fixing what can't be fixed: what's the next thing that depends on this, and does *that* still have room to move? Grieving a missed thing and protecting the next thing are different tasks; don't conflate them.

5. **Explicitly grant the zero-guilt reset from SKILL.md §1.E** before ending the exchange: *"This is done now — not fixed, done. What's next doesn't need to carry this."* Do not ask if they want to talk about why it happened unless they raise it first.

## Ends in

One corrective action taken or explicitly declined, and a clean handoff to whatever's next — not a plan to prevent future misses. Prevention is a SCATTERED-state or ROLLING-state conversation, initiated by the user, on a different day.
