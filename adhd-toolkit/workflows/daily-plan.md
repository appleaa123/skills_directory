# Workflow: Daily Plan

**Route here from**: SCATTERED or STUCK, when the ask is "help me plan today" or "I have too much and don't know when to do what."

## The failure mode this avoids

A default AI response to "plan my day" produces an aspirational schedule: every waking hour filled, back-to-back, assuming perfect focus and zero friction. For ADHD, that schedule is wrong the moment it's written, and every gap between the plan and reality reads as personal failure rather than a bad estimate. **The plan must be built from honest capacity, not from the hours available.**

## Procedure

1. **Ask for real capacity, not calendar hours.** "On a normal day, how many hours of actual focused work do you get, not counting time you're at a desk but not really working?" Most answers land between 2 and 5. Use their number, not 8.

2. **Get the full list out first, unranked.** One line each. Don't let them elaborate on any single item yet — elaboration on item 1 while items 2–8 are still unwritten is how the scattered feeling turns into a plan that only covers a third of what's actually on their mind.

3. **Apply the ADHD Tax Formula to anything with a real deadline**: `Scheduled Time = (Estimated Time × 1.5) + 15 min buffer` (see [cheatsheet.md §3](../cheatsheet.md)). State this once, don't re-derive it per item — just apply it.

4. **Sort into three bins, not a ranked list**: **Today** (must happen, fits in real capacity), **This week** (has a deadline but not today), **Someday** (no deadline — naming it removes it from working memory without requiring it to be scheduled).

5. **Pick the single first thing.** Not the most important — the first. Recommend one default: "Start with [X] because [one-line reason]. Want to run with that, or is something else pulling at you more right now?"

6. **Leave visible slack.** If real capacity is 3 hours, plan 2. The unplanned hour isn't waste — it's where the buffer from step 3 actually gets absorbed, and its absence is why aspirational plans always fail by 2pm.

## Ends in

A **Today** list that fits inside stated real capacity, with one item named as the starting point. Not a full-day schedule — the plan for hour four gets made in hour four.
