A high-density decision matrix and emergency triage guide synthesized from published clinical and educational material on ADHD.

Tools tagged `[practitioner-common]` are widely used in the ADHD community but are **not**
tied to a single documented source — see [sources.md](sources.md). Sections 1–4 are the
operating layer (protocol design); section 5 is `[verified]` documented content.

## 2. The PINCH Motivation Formula `[practitioner-common]`

The documented framing is that the ADHD brain engages through **urgency, challenge,
novelty, and interest** rather than importance `[verified]`. PINCH is a community
mnemonic for that idea, not a documented term.

When facing a low-interest task, inject at least two catalysts:
```
[Motivation] = P (Passion) + I (Interest) + N (Novelty) + C (Competition) + H (Hurry)
```
- **P**: Who does this help? What core value does this serve?
- **I**: What is the most curious or unconventional way to do this?
- **N**: What tool, font, room, or soundtrack can I change?
- **C**: Can I gamify this with a score or personal best?
- **H**: Set a visible 15-minute countdown disc timer now!

## 3. Time Management Quick Rules

- **The ADHD Tax Formula**:
  $$\text{Scheduled Time} = (\text{Estimated Time} \times 1.5) + 15\text{ minutes buffer}$$
- **The "Now vs. Not Now" Rule**: If it is not happening NOW, make it visible (analog clock, visual whiteboard, calendar block).
- **The Point of Performance Rule**: Place tools and reminders where the action physically occurs, never where they "should" be stored.

## 5. Nutritional Psychiatry Cheat Sheet

*Distilled from documented nutritional-psychiatry findings on ADHD `[verified]`. Informational, not medical advice.*

### Foods to Embrace
- **Mediterranean eating pattern**: the single strongest overall documented recommendation for ADHD; low adherence is associated with higher ADHD rates.
- **Polyphenols**: berries, cherries, eggplant, onions, kale, coffee, green tea — antioxidants against the oxidative stress ADHD brains are more prone to.
- **Minerals**: zinc, iron, potassium, magnesium. Zinc deficiency is linked to hyperactivity; children with ADHD show lower iron and magnesium, both used in dopamine synthesis.
- **Vitamins C and B1.**
- **Breakfast**: stimulants blunt morning appetite, so a routine matters. A smoothie beats commercial bars, which are usually sugar and refined carbs.

### Foods to Avoid
- **Gluten** — if you have celiac disease or non-celiac gluten sensitivity. Note you can be sensitive without digestive symptoms.
- **Dairy, specifically A1 milk caseins** — prefer A2, nut, goat's or sheep's milk. In one trial, A1 protein produced slower thinking and more information-processing errors.
- **Caffeine above 400 mg/day** for adults. Caffeine can help attention; overdoing it causes overexcitability. Not recommended for children at any dose.
- **Food colorings and additives** — via the Feingold diet or few foods diet, and only if symptoms don't respond to gentler changes. Meta-analyses estimate **10–30%** of people with ADHD respond.

### One correction worth knowing
> **Sugar does not cause ADHD.** The documented evidence review concludes the popular
> belief is a misperception — if anything, people with ADHD may simply consume more
> sugar. Limiting it is still worthwhile, for general physical and mental health
> rather than as an ADHD intervention.

An earlier version of this file listed sugar as a "focus killer," named specific dyes
(Red 40, Yellow 5/6), halved the caffeine limit, and credited omega-3s with raising
"dopamine receptor density." None of that is documented — see [sources.md](sources.md#1-corrected-claims).
