# Lead Magnets

## When to use it
When advertising your core offer directly isn't converting enough — common for expensive
products or anything that needs explaining before someone buys.

## The method
A **lead magnet** is a *complete solution to a narrow problem*, free or low-cost. Two
requirements:
1. It's valuable enough that you could charge for it.
2. Once solved, it reveals the next problem — which your **core offer** solves for money.

Think of a bar's free salty snacks: they solve hunger (narrow), create thirst, and the drink
revenue more than covers them. Someone who pays with their time now is more likely to pay with
money later.

### Pick the problem
Every problem has a solution; every solution reveals more problems. Choose one that's **narrow
and meaningful**, solve it, and make sure the problem it exposes is one your core offer closes.

### Three types
1. **Reveal a problem** (a diagnosis) — best when the problem worsens the longer it's ignored
   (e.g. an assessment that quantifies what the gap is costing them).
2. **Sample or trial** of the core offer — solve a recurring problem for a short time.
3. **One step of a multi-step process** — deliver step one of something bigger.

### Seven build steps
1. Pick the narrow problem and who it's for.
2. Figure out how to solve it.
3. Figure out how to deliver it.
4. Test the name.
5. Make it easy to consume.
6. Make it genuinely good.
7. Make it easy for them to tell you they want more.

Apply the same "so good it's hard to decline" standard you'd use for a paid offer.

## Steps
1. List the narrow problems that sit *before* the one your core offer solves.
2. Pick one; build a complete, standalone solution to it.
3. Confirm solving it makes the next problem obvious — and that you sell the answer to it.

## Example
A firm that helps founders raise capital builds a free "investor-readiness scorecard." It's
genuinely useful on its own, and completing it makes the real gap obvious ("your metrics story
isn't fundable yet") — which the firm's paid engagement fixes.

## Pitfalls
- A lead magnet that's valuable but doesn't reveal a problem your core offer solves.
- Picking a problem so broad it can't be fully solved in a free resource.
- Overthinking it — narrow and meaningful is the whole rule.

## Metrics & targets
- Try direct core-offer advertising first; add a lead magnet only if that underperforms.
- The lead magnet must stand alone in value *and* set up the next sale.
