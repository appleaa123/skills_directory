# Hiring People to Run Outreach

## When to use it
When your personal capacity to prospect is maxed and the channel — not your effort — is the
limit.

## The method
Getting staff who generate leads is the same skill as getting customers, with the frame changed
from "let potential *customers* know" to "let potential *hires* know." The channels map directly:

| Get customers | Get hires |
|---|---|
| warm outreach | ask your network |
| cold outreach | active recruiting |
| free content | job postings |
| paid ads | promoted job postings |
| customer referrals | employee referrals |
| partners | associations, guilds, professional groups |
| agencies | staffing firms |

**Retention is the hidden constraint.** If you lose a prospector every few weeks, no amount of
recruiting fixes the funnel. Fix churn — incentives, management, onboarding — before scaling
headcount.

**Sequence:** max your own prospecting inputs first, then hand off one channel at a time to
people who can run it profitably on your behalf.

## Steps
1. Max your personal prospecting output first.
2. Run the "internal" version of the four channels to recruit.
3. Fix retention before adding headcount.
4. Hand off one channel at a time.

## Example
A team missed its cold-outreach target two quarters running. Root cause on inspection: a rep was
leaving every four weeks. The fix wasn't more recruiting — it was rep retention. Only after that
was solved did adding headcount multiply output.

## Pitfalls
- Recruiting harder to fill a leaky team.
- Handing off a channel you haven't personally made work yet.

## Metrics & targets
- Once your capacity is maxed, hiring is the next lever — but retention gates it.
