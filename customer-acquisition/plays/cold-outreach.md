# Cold Outreach (one-to-one, cold audience)

## When to use it
When you want to choose exactly who you target, and your warm audience is exhausted. Private
outreach to strangers who haven't given permission.

## The method
Strangers add three problems; solve them in order:

1. **You can't contact them → build a targeted list.** Three sources, in order:
   - **Scraping/data software** — subscribe to several, search by your exact criteria, test a
     few hundred from each; if the contact info is current and the people match the claim, take
     as many as it will give.
     Then move on if the fit is wrong.
   - **List brokers** — pay someone to assemble a targeted list.
   - **Manual research** — build it yourself if the first two fail.
   Precision is the payoff: you can specify "operations directors at 50–200-person manufacturers
   in the Midwest" and only talk to them.
2. **They don't know or trust you → personalize, then lead with big fast value.** Open with
   something specific to them, then give a genuinely useful result, resource, or insight *before*
   any ask. Use acknowledge–affirm–ask once they reply.
3. **They ignore you → volume and variety.** Expect low response rates. Compensate with more
   attempts, more channels (email, text, DM, call, mail), more touches. Use automation.

## Steps
1. Build the list: software → brokers → manual. Test a sample before scaling.
2. Write an opener: personalized hook + big fast value, no ask.
3. Run high volume across multiple channels with follow-ups; automate the repetitive parts.

## Example
An agency targets a scraped list of exactly-fit e-commerce operators. Opener: "Saw your site's
checkout drops mobile users on step 2 — here's a 3-line fix and a teardown of the flow. No ask,
just useful." Follow up across email and LinkedIn, three touches, then move on.

## Pitfalls
- Skipping the list-quality test and scaling a bad list.
- Leading with a pitch instead of value.
- Expecting warm-outreach response rates from cold contacts.

## Metrics & targets
- It's a numbers game: find how many touches it takes to engage one prospect, then do more.
- Targeting precision is the reason to use this channel — use it.
