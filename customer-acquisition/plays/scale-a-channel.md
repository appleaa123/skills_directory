# Scale a Channel

## When to use it
Once a channel works "to some degree," and especially when growth has plateaued and you think
you've "tapped the market."

## The method
Scale in three moves, in order:

**1. More.** Double your inputs — twice the outreach, twice the content, twice the ad spend.
Even with zero improvement, more volume produces more engaged prospects. Operationalize this
with a daily action commitment (`daily-advertising-plan`).

**2. Better.** You only improve by **testing**. Push volume until the channel "breaks" (cost per
acquisition becomes unsustainable), then find and fix the **biggest drop-off point** — the step
where the most prospects fall out, where a small improvement produces the largest result gain.
Fix one, hold it, move to the next. Thousands of small tests separate the people who scale from
the people who don't.

Example funnel: 30% opt in → **5% apply** ← biggest drop-off → 50% schedule. Improving the 5%
step beats improving any other step by a wide margin.

**3. New.** When returns from more-and-better fall below what a new placement, platform, or
channel would produce, expand — in this order: **new placement → new platform → new channel**
(most leads for the least work).

**Watch for the market-size illusion:** you advertise with one channel, on one platform, in one
style, to one narrow slice — and assume the few competitors you see there split the whole
market. The real market is far larger. Most businesses stay small because, at a plateau, "I'm as
big as I can get" is easier to say than "I'm not as good at this as I thought."

## Steps
1. Do far more of what already works.
2. When it breaks, map your funnel step by step, find the biggest drop-off, test there until it
   frees up, then hold and repeat on the next one.
3. Only after more-and-better are exhausted, add: a new placement, then a new platform, then a
   new channel.

## Example
A company at $2M/year on one ad platform, no content, no cold outreach, not tracking
click-to-close. Doubling ad spend and fixing the application-form drop-off (the biggest leak)
roughly doubled leads before any "new" channel was needed — the niche was nowhere near
saturated.

## Pitfalls
- Jumping to "new" before exhausting "more" and "better" — new is much harder in practice.
- Testing random steps instead of the biggest drop-off.
- Believing the plateau instead of the math.

## Metrics & targets
- More and better reinforce each other: do more until it breaks, hold, fix the drop-off, repeat.
