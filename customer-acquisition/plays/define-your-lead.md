# Define Your Lead

## When to use it
At the start — before you measure anything or build a channel. Getting the definitions right
changes what you optimize for.

## The method
- A **contact** is a person you can reach. A bought email list, your phone contacts, a scraped
  database, people on the street — all contacts.
- An **engaged prospect** is a contact who has *shown interest* in what you sell: gave their
  details on your site, followed you (and is reachable), replied to a campaign, booked a call,
  walked in.

Engaged prospects are the true output of advertising. Count those, not impressions or list size.
Every method and every optimization in this playbook is measured by engaged prospects produced.

A useful mindset: if someone is making more money than you, they're better at some part of this
game — which is good news, because you can learn it. Don't assume shortcuts or broken rules;
those beliefs don't make you better.

## Steps
1. Decide, explicitly, what counts as an engaged prospect in your business (the specific action).
2. Instrument that so you can count it per channel.
3. Judge every channel and test by engaged prospects, not raw reach.

## Example
A B2B service defines an engaged prospect as "replied to an outreach message with a question,
or booked a call." A 5,000-name list isn't 5,000 leads; the 40 people who replied this week are.

## Pitfalls
- Optimizing for contacts (reachability) instead of engagement (demonstrated interest).
- Reporting vanity metrics — impressions, followers — that don't predict revenue.

## Metrics & targets
- One clear engaged-prospect definition, counted per channel.
