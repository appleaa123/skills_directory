# Customer Referrals

## When to use it
Build this first — you already generate customers through the four channels, and referrals are
the cheapest, best-fit leads you'll get. Target **25% or more** of new customers from referrals.

## The method
Referrals come from goodwill, and goodwill comes from *more value*, not a lower price. The
question is never "how do we discount?" but "how do we give more?"

Six ways to earn more referrals, mapped to the parts of an ad:
1. **Better hooks → sell better-fit customers.** Customers who get the most value have the most
   goodwill and refer most. Study your *lowest-churn* customers for what they share, then target
   more of them.
2. **Set better expectations** — align what you promise with what you deliver.
3. **Raise confidence → get more people to a real result.**
4. **Cut time to result → faster wins.**
5. **Cut effort → keep improving the product.**
6. **Tell them what to buy (and refer) next.**

Add an explicit referral program with real incentives, and mechanisms to ask at the moment of
peak goodwill.

## Steps
1. Identify your lowest-churn customer profile; shift targeting toward it.
2. Tighten expectations, speed, and effort so more customers get a real result.
3. Launch an incentivized referral program and ask at peak-goodwill moments.

## Example
A services firm with high churn examined its lowest-churn customers — all in one sub-niche
raising outside funding. It refocused on that niche (even though they were only 15% of current
customers). Fewer, better-fit customers → less churn → more goodwill → more referrals.

## Pitfalls
- Letting product quality slip while scaling — it's the referral engine, and most people never
  recover from this.
- Trying to buy referrals with discounts instead of earning them with value.

## Metrics & targets
- Aim for ≥25% of new customers from referrals.
- Referrals keep coming even when paid channels are switched off.
