# Paid Ads (one-to-many, cold audience)

## When to use it
To scale reach fast, once you can fund the learning curve. You pay a platform for access to
strangers' attention.

## The method
An ad has three parts: **attention hook → value → call to action.**

### 1. Attention hook — get the right person to notice (the highest-leverage part)
Each second of the ad exists to sell the next second, and the opening does most of the work. Put
most of your effort into the first few seconds and test them the most. A hook can be
hyper-specific (names one person) or broad (gets everyone) — aim specific enough to catch the
right people, broad enough to catch many. **Labels** work well: words a group identifies with
("independent pharmacy owners," "remote-first teams"). "Local area + type of person" is a
durable pattern.

### 2. Value — three lenses that together answer *why they should care*
- **Benefit:** know the handful of ways your product moves each value driver (desired result,
  confidence, speed, effort) — and how it helps them avoid each opposite.
- **Stakeholders:** everyone whose opinion affects your buyer's standing — partner, kids,
  colleagues, boss, peers, competitors. Show status gained if they buy, lost if they don't, from
  each viewpoint.
- **Timeline:** run the consequences through the buyer's past, present, and future (and other
  people's timelines too).
Long ads just cover more angles than short ads; keep the hook and CTA the same, add or drop
middle angles per platform.

### 3. Call to action — say exactly what to do
Spell it out: click the button, call the number, reply YES, scan the code. High motivation lasts
a very short time — capture it. Make it frictionless.

### Funding it: customer-funded acquisition
Measure ads by lifetime gross profit ÷ cost to acquire a customer.
- **Testing a new ad that IS producing leads:** let spend run to about **2× the cash a customer
  generates in the first 30 days** before killing it.
- **A new ad producing NO leads:** kill it before **1× that 30-day cash.**
- **Once ads break even or better:** stop asking "how much should I spend?" and ask "how many
  customers can I handle?" Reverse the budget from that number and pad ~20% for scaling
  inefficiency.
- Expect to lose money while building the machine; judge returns over a long horizon, not next
  week.

## Steps
1. Write 3+ hooks; test them hardest.
2. Build the value section from the three lenses; scale length by angle count.
3. End with one explicit, easy CTA.
4. Judge on lifetime-profit-to-acquisition-cost over months; test to ~2× 30-day cash.

## Example
A B2B tool: hook — "Finance teams at 50–200-person companies: your month-end close is 3 days
too long." Value — the benefit (close in 1 day), the stakeholders (CFO stops chasing you, the
board stops asking), the timeline (last quarter's scramble vs. next quarter calm). CTA — "Book a
15-minute teardown."

## Pitfalls
- Spreading effort evenly instead of loading the first few seconds.
- Judging an ad on one week of data.
- Scaling spend before the unit economics work.

## Metrics & targets
- Lifetime gross profit : acquisition cost, over a long horizon.
- Test to ~2× first-30-day cash; kill no-lead ads before 1×.
