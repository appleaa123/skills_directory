# Warm Outreach (one-to-one, warm audience)

## When to use it
Day one, or any time you need leads fast. Reaching out privately to people who already gave you
permission to contact them — friends, family, followers, current and past customers, contacts.
The cheapest source with the highest response rate, and where every business starts.

## The method
Five steps:
1. **Build the list** — everyone you can contact: phone contacts, email, social followers, past
   customers. Whether it's 100 names or 100,000, use all of them.
2. **Warm the relationship** — you haven't asked for anything yet, so just reconnect and provide
   value. Check their recent activity for a genuine hook: "Saw you launched the new location —
   congrats. How's it going?"
3. **Personalize the message** — no copy-paste blasts.
4. **Set a fixed daily reach-out count and hit it every working day** — call, text, email, DM,
   mail — up to 3 touches each (once a day for three days, once a week for physical mail, or
   until they reply). 100/day is the anchor; scale it to your deal size — a high-ticket B2B
   service might commit to 20–40 genuinely personalized touches a day, a high-volume motion
   to 100+. The count matters less than never missing a day.
5. **When they reply, use acknowledge–affirm–ask:**
   - **Acknowledge** — restate what they said in your own words (shows you listened).
   - **Affirm** — a genuine compliment, ideally tied to a positive trait.
   - **Ask** — another question that steers toward your offer's topic ("Are you still finding
     time to train?" / "Who's handling that for you right now?").

## Steps
1. Compile every contact into one list.
2. Batch personalized reconnect messages, value-first, no ask.
3. Pick a daily reach-out count you can sustain (100 anchor, scaled to deal size) and hit it
   every working day, 3 touches each.
4. Run acknowledge–affirm–ask on every reply, toward your offer's topic.

## Example
A bookkeeper messages 100 old contacts a day: "Hey — saw you took over the family business,
that's big. How's the first year going?" Reply: "Busy, drowning in admin." → acknowledge ("first
year running everything, that's a lot") → affirm ("and you're still growing it, that takes
grit") → ask ("is the bookkeeping side under control, or is that part of the drowning?").

## Pitfalls
- Leading with the ask instead of reconnecting first.
- Generic template messages.
- Stopping at one touch — most replies come on the second or third.

## Metrics & targets
- A fixed daily reach-out count (100 anchor, scaled to deal size), 3 touches each, never missed.
- Highest response rate of the four channels — exhaust it before leaning on cold methods.
