# Free Content (one-to-many, warm audience)

## When to use it
To build the asset that compounds. Publishing publicly so many people learn about what you sell
at once. The content itself disappears; the **audience** is what keeps growing — that's the
compounding asset.

## The method
Every piece of content has three jobs:
1. **Get noticed** — a headline, first few seconds, or thumbnail that stops the right person.
   Nothing else matters if they don't stop.
2. **Keep them watching** — lists, steps, stories, open loops that pull them through.
3. **Pay it off** — deliver on the reason they engaged. The audience decides whether it was
   worth it, not you.

**Give far more than you ask.** Publish free value repeatedly; the audience will ask to buy when
they're ready. Include calls to action, but keep the ratio heavily toward giving.

**Topic sources:** lessons from your distant past (connect the lesson to what you sell), things
you did recently (mine your calendar and meetings), what's happening now, trending subjects, and
deliberately created demonstrations. Personal experience differentiates automatically — no one
else has lived your work.

## Steps
1. Pick a topic from one of the five sources, aimed at a problem your buyer has.
2. Draft it as get-noticed → keep-watching → pay-off.
3. Publish as often as you can sustain; the audience compounds even as posts fade.
4. Weave in occasional, low-pressure calls to action.

## Example
A fintech founder posts a short lesson: opening line — "I lost a week's revenue to a payment
bug I'd been warned about." Middle — the three checks that would have caught it. Payoff — a
reusable pre-launch checklist. Relatable, useful, and it connects to what the company sells.

## Pitfalls
- Treating the content as the asset instead of the audience.
- Asking for the sale more than you give.
- Waiting for the "secret format" instead of publishing volume.

## Metrics & targets
- Structure every piece as get-noticed → keep-watching → pay-off.
- Give many times for every ask.
- Free content also retains paying customers and warms cold audiences for the other channels.
