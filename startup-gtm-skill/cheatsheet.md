# Cheatsheet

## Decision Rules

- **Narrative order is inductive.** Problem → who has it → cost → status quo & why it fails → what changed now → how it works → proof. If a prospect disagrees at any step, stop there.
- **Charge from day one.** Never go unpriced. "Sure, easy" with no hesitation = underpriced, raise it. Nodding through the pitch then blanching at price = priced to perfection, back off slightly — that's near the ceiling.
- **Target pain, not availability.** Never prospect friends/incubator-mates without the pain — burns time, produces churny bad-fit customers.
- **Hardest gate picks the sourcing path.** Required title → people-centric search. Required tech/industry → company-centric search.
- **Deer, not rabbits or elephants**, for the first ICP tier — big enough to fund the pain, small enough to decide fast. Don't elephant-hunt for deal size alone.
- **Negotiate in strict priority order:** (1) cash up front, (2) contract length, (3) everything else (price/unit, auto-renew, opt-outs). Retreat one lever at a time; trade a concession on one for a gain on another.
- **Objections = engagement, not rejection.** Catch it → surface the real question → answer with quant+qual proof → confirm → return to close. Best handling happens upstream in discovery, not as a comeback line.
- **Qualify the account, not the lead.** An inbound contact from an unqualified org isn't a demo obligation; an unqualified contact at a qualified org gets redirected internally.
- **Respond to inbound in minutes, not hours** — interest decays almost immediately.
- **Two-stage hiring gate.** First sales hire only after *you* have personally closed a statistically significant number of deals. A dedicated sales leader/VP only after 1–3 of your own-hired reps are already repeatably successful without you — don't let a leader "figure it out" for you.
- **Premature scaling kills companies; lagged scaling only costs opportunity.** Between the two, err toward proving the motion longer.
- **Mindset defaults:** plenty over scarcity (cut marginal opps fast), activity over polish (call now, move on), record everything for "future you," assume the sale is inevitable, expect to win but stay unfazed by losing most of the time.

## Trade-off Matrices

**Account sizing tiers**

| Tier | Decision speed | Risk |
|---|---|---|
| Rabbits (minnows) | Fast | High churn — process too immature to use the tool well |
| Deer (dolphins) — **target** | Fast enough, real budget | Best fit; bias to the bigger end within tier |
| Elephants (whales) | Slow, legacy-laden | Concentration risk on one client |

**Negotiation asks → response**

| Prospect wants | Founder move |
|---|---|
| Lower per-unit price | Only for more volume or longer term |
| Lower total (fixed budget) | Cut seats and raise per-unit price, or shorten term |
| Shorter duration/pilot | Charge 2–3x normal per-period rate |
| Split/quarterly payments | Raise total price (AR risk + cost of capital), e.g. $10k upfront → $12k quarterly |

**Renewal ownership**

| Model | Trade-off |
|---|---|
| CS owns implementation + QBRs + renewal | Simple, but renewal can be under-sold as "just admin" |
| CS owns implementation/QBRs; separate Commercial Account Manager closes renewal | Specialized; avoids the common failure of an AE resurfacing only ~60 days before renewal after 270+ silent days |

## Thresholds & Defaults

- ICP first list: **50–100 target accounts**.
- Implementation fee rule of thumb: **3x** the fully-loaded labor cost.
- Cold drip: email #2 beats #1 (**18% vs 12%**), holds through #5, then declines. Full 7-email personalized drip ≈ **15x** a single one-off email.
- Inbound speed: **100x drop-off** in contact rate 5-min vs 30-min response; **10x** between 5 and 10 min.
- Inbound persistence: each added call attempt adds **~15%** contact probability, flattening after attempt 6, for **~90% cumulative** odds by then.
- Win rate: **15–30%** reliably is the signal you're ready to add reps; below that, fix messaging/product/pricing before scaling.
- Cost of sales target: fully-loaded AE+SDR(+SE) cost ≤ **20–25%** of revenue closed (e.g. a $100k rep should book ~**$500k**/yr).
- Manager span of control: **1:5 vs 1:8** reps — 1:8 cuts per-rep managerial cost **~40%**.
- QBR/renewal cadence: QBRs **quarterly**. Final QBR ~**3 months** before term end → renewal call ~**6 weeks** out, calendared when the final QBR closes. Second-to-last QBR should already tell you if they'll renew; extend **1–2 months** unpaid to fix a showstopper. Report "to be scheduled" QBRs **60 days** out.
- CS health signal: account logged in / hit a measurable outcome in the last **30 days**.
- Closed-lost resurrection default: **60 days** absent explicit timing.
- Pipeline hygiene flags: **no activity 7 days** (down-funnel), **stuck in-stage 2+ weeks**, **no future meeting on calendar**.
- Churn math: **5% monthly churn roughly halves** 58-month MRR vs. 0% churn; **2.5% monthly expansion** instead nearly doubles it.
- Onboarding attrition baseline: industry loses **30–50%** of a hiring class within 6 months, mostly from bad onboarding.

## Tells & Smells

- Prospect gives a vague "not sure it's a fit" → it's a masked objection; force it open with "what specifically is blocking us from progressing right now?"
- "No need" objection with a squishy, non-specific answer → really budget, authority, or priority hiding underneath — invite honesty directly.
- Founder winning **every** deal, or personally out-closing his own reps → not a sign of great selling; it's a thin/cherry-picked pipeline, or proof the rep pool needs to grow (4 average reps beat 1 star founder on total output).
- Broken "micro-contracts" (missed calls, hard-to-schedule next meeting) → deal is stalling; keep a dated next action on the calendar so ambiguity surfaces early.
- A founder hiring a full VP of Sales + SDR/AE pod before closing "a few dozen" deals solo → premature scaling, the anti-pattern that most commonly kills early-stage go-to-market.
