# Patterns

## Build Many Shallow Relationships
**When to use**: Onboarding a first-time seller.
**How**: Expect dozens of net-new weekly interactions and 100+ concurrent pipeline conversations, versus the dozen deep relationships professionals normally hold. Offset with rigorous CRM notes for "future you."
**Trade-offs**: Without note-taking discipline, contacts blur together and become unmanageable.

## Inductive Narrative-Building Sequence
**When to use**: Drafting a sales narrative from scratch.
**How**: Walk problem, who has it, cost, status-quo failure, what changed now, how it works, proof, in order; if a prospect disagrees at a step, stop there.
**Trade-offs**: Slower than jumping to "how it works," but skipping ahead builds a pitch that won't survive pushback.

## Underpriced-and-Iterate Pricing
**When to use**: Setting initial pricing with no comparable or ROI model.
**How**: Start deliberately underpriced, raise price whenever a prospect shows zero hesitation, and grandfather existing customers at the old rate when you do.
**Trade-offs**: Leaves money on the table early, but pricing "to perfection" risks sticker shock, harder to diagnose later.

## Rabbits / Deer / Elephants Account Sizing
**When to use**: Prioritizing which accounts to target first.
**How**: Classify accounts as rabbits (fast, small, high churn), elephants (huge deals, entrenched process, concentration risk), or deer (real pain, fast decisions) — target deer first, biased toward the bigger end.
**Trade-offs**: Elephants look attractive on paper; chasing them early risks becoming a services shop for one account.

## Account-First vs. Contact-First Sourcing
**When to use**: Deciding where to start a prospect list.
**How**: Identify the hardest gate — a required title (people-centric: search LinkedIn, roll up to companies) or a required stack/industry (company-centric: filter firmographic data, then find people).
**Trade-offs**: Picking the wrong start wastes list-building effort; the gate should decide direction, not preference.

## Personalized Click-Target Cold Email
**When to use**: Drafting outreach materials.
**How**: Every cold email needs a subject line customized to the prospect, plain pain documentation (name the pain, never the product category), a hyperlinked click target for tracking, and one CTA — 100% text, no images.
**Trade-offs**: Designed emails read as spam-blast; this level of personalization doesn't scale without templating.

## Drip Cadence with Breakup Email
**When to use**: Structuring an outbound sequence.
**How**: Spread the narrative across short emails alternated with calls (email+call day 1, call day 2, call+voicemail day 3, email day 5, breakup email day 7).
**Trade-offs**: Needs enough content for multiple touches; one email underperforms a 7-email drip by ~15x.

## Business-Context-First Demo
**When to use**: Structuring any live demo.
**How**: Open with what you already know about this account (careers page, funding, stack), then walk the deck's messaging buckets in importance order, following the prospect's real workflow, not a feature tour.
**Trade-offs**: Requires real pre-call research; a non-contextual demo "smells like" a canned performance.

## 5-Minute Inbound Response Rule
**When to use**: Handling inbound demo requests.
**How**: Respond within 5 minutes where possible (contact rates drop 10x by 10 min, 100x by 30); pair auto-response with a callback number, and persist ~6 attempts (~90% cumulative odds).
**Trade-offs**: Demands near-constant availability; a chat widget promising instant response but not delivering damages trust.

## Hard Qualifier Email
**When to use**: An inbound lead looks likely unqualified.
**How**: Send a short email naming who the product is for and why this account may not fit, asking them to correct you if wrong. Qualify the account, not the lead — pivot an unqualified contact toward the right person instead of disqualifying outright.
**Trade-offs**: Needs enough public signal to guess informedly; a wrong guess reads as presumptuous.

## Foreshortened Demo for Authority Objections
**When to use**: A convinced champion says a boss/CFO must sign off.
**How**: Confirm authority is the only remaining blocker, then run point on booking and co-presenting to the real decision-maker as a second, shorter, ROI-focused demo.
**Trade-offs**: Adds a meeting and cycle time; easier if the decision-maker was identified earlier, in discovery.

## Status-Quo Cost Reveal
**When to use**: "We're happy with how we do it now" objections.
**How**: Make the cost of inaction concrete with a dollar figure tied to metrics the buyer's stakeholders already track, layered with FOMO about competitors adopting.
**Trade-offs**: Needs a defensible cost model from the ROI section; an unquantified claim won't move a status-quo buyer.

## Cash-First Negotiation Priority
**When to use**: Any negotiation over price, term, or payment schedule.
**How**: Prioritize cash up front, then contract length, then everything else. Retreat one increment at a time; when conceding one lever, pull a different one back.
**Trade-offs**: Rigid adherence can lose a deal that would close on a different trade; an easy 5% discount is often cheaper than defending it.

## Shorter-Duration Price Markup (2–3x)
**When to use**: A prospect wants a shorter term or split payments.
**How**: Price the shorter or split option at 2–3x the normal per-period rate, justified by fixed onboarding costs amortizing over less time and AR/cost-of-capital risk.
**Trade-offs**: Pushback follows if the rationale isn't stated plainly — otherwise it reads as punitive.

## Bottom-Up Pipeline Triage
**When to use**: Recurring pipeline review.
**How**: Review bottom-up — contracts out and verbal agreements first, then proposals, then earlier stages — weighting time by deal size, closing stale opps via a breakup email.
**Trade-offs**: A 10–20% per-deal win rate (30% excellent) is normal for a new-market solution; winning everything signals over-qualification.

## One-and-Done Implementation Call
**When to use**: Onboarding a newly closed customer — the most integral CS mechanism.
**How**: Run implementation off the sales deck, one-on-one not group, in one 60-minute call where the user executes actions live in-product. End with a back-check meeting ~2 weeks out tied to measurable homework.
**Trade-offs**: Group calls save time but diffuse accountability; skipping the back-check plants a "churn time bomb."

## Proactive Usage Monitoring
**When to use**: Post-implementation, catching churn risk before renewal.
**How**: Inspect usage on a recurring cadence (log in "as" the customer, pull custom reports) rather than waiting for tickets; reach out when usage drops or a user leaves the org.
**Trade-offs**: Needs product/engineering investment; without it, success work stays reactive until renewal.

## Cost-of-Sales 20–25% Rule
**When to use**: Deciding whether a sales motion is efficient enough to scale.
**How**: Target AE/SDR cost at no more than 20–25% of revenue closed; use a 15–30% demo-to-close win rate as the scaling-readiness signal (distinct from per-deal pipeline win rate).
**Trade-offs**: A win rate below 15% signals a messaging or pricing problem — fix that before adding reps.

## Sales Pod Maturity Ladder
**When to use**: Deciding what to hire next while scaling.
**How**: Progress through staged exit criteria — Founder Doing It All, Founder+SDR/CSM, Founder+SDR+2 AEs, Initial Sales Pod, Pod Abstraction/Scale — proving repeatability before adding specialization.
**Trade-offs**: Bounded by premature scaling (headcount before proof) and lagged scaling (a proven motion left too long).

## Cascading Screening Funnel
**When to use**: Hiring any sales role, especially the first.
**How**: Sequence cheapest-and-most-disqualifying-first — async written screen (~12 questions), homework/voicemail pitch, phone screen, mock presentation for closing roles — before team interviews.
**Trade-offs**: Slower than one interview; skipping stages burns team time on candidates who should've been filtered earlier.

## Cohort-Based Onboarding University
**When to use**: Onboarding new sales hires.
**How**: Run a fixed 1–2 week "university" in cohorts: admin, culture, market/business/technical grounding, product & presentation training, drilling (group, sparring, ride-alongs), then bluebirding inbound with a senior rep as backup.
**Trade-offs**: Cohorts amortize instructor effort but need batched hires; a solo hire loses built-in sparring partners.

## Exit-Criteria Gating for Management Hires
**When to use**: Deciding when to hire a sales manager or VP of Sales.
**How**: Don't hire management until non-founder reps sell repeatably at least as well as the founder did — the founder is the only one who's cracked the motion well enough to teach it.
**Trade-offs**: Delays professionalization, but hiring early risks a lossy "game of telephone" institutionalizing an unproven process.
