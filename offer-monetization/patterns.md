# Patterns — Offer Monetization

Each structure as a compact play: what it is · when to use · how · trade-offs.

## Attraction offers (turn strangers into customers)

### Results-based refund offer
**When:** entry offer for things people start and quit; new, current, or lapsed customers.
**How:** deposit, refunded (cash or credit) if they get the result, do the actions, or both.
Make results/actions trivially trackable; structure required touchpoints as offer opportunities.
**Trade-offs:** 100% discount; still profits because few complete everything and finishers
reinvest.

### Prize giveaway offer
**When:** many pre-qualified leads for the flagship.
**How:** advertise a prize (= your core offer, with a value anchor) for contact info +
qualifying actions; deadline; pick a winner; sell all non-winners at a discount (~10–30% of
margin).
**Trade-offs:** revenue is in the non-winner follow-up — plan it first.

### Basic-vs-premium side-by-side
**When:** almost any business; when price-sensitive leads would walk.
**How:** advertise the basic version; on contact show basic + premium, emphasize premium. Build
basic by subtracting from premium.
**Trade-offs:** basic-takers need an upgrade path; don't hard-sell basic.

### Buy more, get some free
**When:** things bought in quantity; collecting cash up front; extending tenure.
**How:** free ≥ paid in value; more free than paid; raise base prices to fund it.
**Trade-offs:** some payment breakage; price rise may need to be seasonal.

### Pay now or pay later
**When:** pulling cash forward; "free to start" hooks.
**How:** $0 card-on-file "pay later, only if satisfied" vs. 20–50%-off "pay now" + bonuses.
Capture the card, follow up to convert to pay-now. Line up an upsell for after.
**Trade-offs:** you trade margin now for cash now.

## Upsell offers (more cash, fast)

### Next-problem upsell
**When:** your core offer creates an obvious next problem.
**How:** "you can't have X without Y," offered at the moment of purchase. Map the problem chain
first.
**Trade-offs:** needs a genuine next problem.

### Guided-menu upsell
**When:** you have multiple offers/tiers.
**How:** de-select what they don't need → prescribe what they do → A vs. B → card on file.
**Trade-offs:** requires building the menu first.

### Price-anchored step-down
**When:** the cheaper option shares the premium's core function.
**How:** present the priciest option first → expect the reaction → "do you care about [premium
part]?" → offer the cheaper one.
**Trade-offs:** budget for the reaction; occasional buyer takes the anchor (upside).

### Credit-forward offer
**When:** lapsed customers, refund requests, competitors' unhappy customers, regulars.
**How:** credit some/all prior spend toward the next offer. Decide amount, then who/what/how.
**Trade-offs:** you forgo the credit's cash value but keep the customer and revenue stream.

## Downsell offers (turn no into yes)

### Payment plan
**When:** "I can't afford it," one-time / physical purchases.
**How:** descend the ladder — full → half now/half payday → thirds → "what can you pay?" Time
charges to pay dates. Take the biggest deposit possible.
**Trade-offs:** card breakage; only a win if it nets more customers.

### Conditional free trial
**When:** recurring products/services where the customer must do work.
**How:** card + commitment; state activation terms (reuse the refund-offer criteria) and the
penalty fee. Break the fee up by criterion, or one lump fee if a single miss is fatal.
**Trade-offs:** only works in do-the-work businesses; "$1 first month" beats "free" for card
validity.

### Feature-reduction downsell
**When:** after a "no" to a prescribed/premium offer.
**How:** remove features highest value first, drop the price, ask "how about now?" Either they
take the cheaper deal, or the contrast re-sells them the original.
**Trade-offs:** strip too much and the offer stops solving the problem.

## Continuity offers (keep them paying)

### Sign-up-bonus subscription
**When:** converting front-end buyers into members.
**How:** on the sales floor — "want it free? become a member" → members-only extras (worth >
first payment) → prepaid discount upsell.
**Trade-offs:** bonuses cost you — make them cheap to deliver at scale.

### Free-time subscription discount
**When:** you want term commitments; tune by churn history.
**How:** free time frontloaded (more sign-ups, more churn), earned/backloaded (fewer, less
churn), or spread (cash flows). Extend the term, don't shorten it. Cancel = repay the discount.
Bill every 4 weeks (+8.3%).
**Trade-offs:** frontloaded discounts delay 30-day payback.

### Commitment fee waiver
**When:** month-to-month businesses wanting longer tenure.
**How:** startup fee = 3–5× monthly; waived for a term commitment; cancel early → owe the fee.
**Trade-offs:** a high fee suppresses month-to-month sign-ups (often acceptable).

## The build sequence
Attraction (cover costs, get customers reliably) → Upsell (30-day profit well above acquisition
+ delivery) → Downsell (convert the no's; alternate how-they-pay and what-they-get) → Continuity
(one more sale in the 30-day window + recurring cash). Make each stage reliable — financially
and operationally — before the next.
