# Cheatsheet — Offer Monetization

## The one number
**30-day gross profit per customer** vs. **cost to get + serve that customer.**
- profit ≥ cost within 30 days → good model
- profit ≥ cost of acquiring/serving 2+ more customers within 30 days → cash no longer limits growth
- why 30 days: one cycle of interest-free card float — acquire → collect → pay off → repeat

## Which offer do I reach for?

| Goal / situation | Offer | Role |
|---|---|---|
| Convert ads to customers + cash | Basic-vs-premium / results-based refund | Attraction |
| Steepest discount, drives completion | Results-based refund offer | Attraction |
| Many pre-qualified leads for the flagship | Prize giveaway offer | Attraction |
| Collect cash up front, extend tenure | Buy more, get some free | Attraction |
| Pull future cash forward | Pay now or pay later | Attraction |
| Core offer creates an obvious next problem | Next-problem upsell ("can't have X without Y") | Upsell |
| You have several offers/tiers | Guided-menu upsell | Upsell |
| Cheaper option shares the core function | Price-anchored step-down | Upsell |
| Win back lapsed / unhappy customers; avoid a refund | Credit-forward offer | Upsell |
| "I can't afford it" (one-time / physical) | Payment plan | Downsell |
| "I can't afford it" (recurring, do-the-work) | Conditional free trial | Downsell |
| Need to charge less | Feature-reduction downsell | Downsell |
| Convert front-end buyers to members | Sign-up-bonus subscription | Continuity |
| Want term commitments | Free-time subscription discount | Continuity |
| Reduce churn on month-to-month | Commitment fee waiver | Continuity |

## Decision rules
- Build order: attraction → upsell → downsell → continuity. Make each stage reliable (financial
  + operational) before the next. Never start with a "finished" model.
- Each stage funds the next — don't skip ahead.
- Refund request → offer a credit-forward first; if they insist, give the cash back.
- Downsell: keep price, change **how they pay** (payment plan / trial) — or lower price, change
  **what they get** (feature reduction). Alternate both in one conversation.
- Feature reduction: remove features **highest value first** so people re-sell themselves.
- Subscription discount: frontload = more sign-ups + more churn; backload = fewer + less churn;
  spread = cash flows. Pick by churn history.
- When the model starts working, operations break — hire the operator early.

## Thresholds & heuristics
- Commitment fee ≈ 3–5× the monthly rate.
- Bill every 4 weeks, not monthly → 13 cycles/yr → ~+8.3% revenue, ~+41% profit at 20% margin.
- Buy-more-get-free: more free than paid; actually raise base prices to fund it.
- Price-anchored step-down: show the most expensive option first, always.
- Giveaway prize = the exact thing you want everyone to buy, with a stated value anchor;
  consolation discount ≈ 10–30% of margin.
- Sign-up bonus value > first recurring payment.
- Finding the attraction offer that works can take months.
- Continuity timing can be after the first 30 days — that's fine.

## Compounding
2× revenue per customer × 2× customers × 2× payment speed → ~8× faster growth. 3× each → ~27×.

## Combine them
Example sequence: basic-vs-premium → next-problem upsell (annual tier) + sign-up bonus →
payment-plan ladder + frontloaded subscription discount → guided-menu upsell + feature-reduction
downsell to a minimum package.
One clever combination — "reduced rate now, that rate for life" — can be attraction, upsell, and
continuity at once.
