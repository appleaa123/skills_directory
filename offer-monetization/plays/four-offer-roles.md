# The Four Offer Roles

## When to use it
Deciding what to offer next, or diagnosing why revenue per customer is low. Making more offers
that people buy makes more money; combining offers in a sequence is what builds a
fast-payback model.

## The method
Four roles, each doing a different job:

1. **Attraction offer** — turns strangers into customers. Something free or discounted that
   converts advertising spend into cash and customers.
2. **Upsell offer** — gets people to spend more, immediately. Usually more, better, or newer
   versions of what they just bought.
3. **Downsell offer** — gets a "no" to become a "yes" by changing the price or the terms.
4. **Continuity offer** — keeps people paying on a recurring basis until they cancel.

Why all four, in order:
- No attraction offer → not enough customers.
- Only an attraction offer → little revenue per customer.
- Add an upsell → real cash starts arriving.
- Many still say no → downsells recover them.
- Add continuity → that revenue recurs every period.

Offers can be used alone, in multiples of one role, or all four combined, in whatever order fits
the customer's problem sequence.

## Steps
1. Identify which roles you currently have. Most stuck businesses have only an attraction offer,
   or only a core offer with nothing after it.
2. Add the missing role that most directly lifts 30-day cash — usually an upsell first.
3. Then a downsell to recover the no's, then continuity.

## Example
An e-commerce brand sells a $40 starter kit (attraction), offers a $25 refill bundle at checkout
(upsell), a single $15 refill for people who decline the bundle (downsell), and a
subscribe-and-save auto-ship (continuity). Same product line, four roles, far more per customer.

## Pitfalls
- Confusing the roles — an upsell is not a downsell; each has a distinct job.
- Adding continuity before acquisition and upsells are reliable.
- Copying another company's exact sequence instead of matching it to your customer's problems.

## Metrics & targets
- A single offer with a profit can sustain a business; the sequence is what makes it scale.
