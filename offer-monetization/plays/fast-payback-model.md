# The Fast-Payback Model

## When to use it
Whenever acquisition costs more than a customer generates in the near term, payback takes
months, or growth is limited by cash rather than demand.

## The method
Arrange your offers so that the gross profit from one customer exceeds the cost to **get and
serve** that customer **within about 30 days**.

- **Good model:** 30-day gross profit per customer > 30-day cost to acquire + serve them.
- **Strong model:** 30-day gross profit per customer ≥ the cost to acquire and serve **two or
  more** additional customers. At this point cash is no longer the constraint on growth.

Why 30 days: it's roughly one cycle of interest-free credit. Acquire a customer, collect,
clear the balance, acquire the next one — repeat indefinitely.

**The trap this breaks:** normally the slow accumulation of small profits from many customers
eventually pays for one new customer, so you can only afford to acquire in bulk once you already
operate at scale (or have outside funding). Pulling cash forward removes that dependency.

**The compounding:** improve three things together — revenue per customer, number of customers,
and how fast they pay — and growth compounds. Doubling all three ≈ 8× faster; tripling ≈ 27×.

## Steps
1. Calculate today's 30-day gross profit per customer and today's 30-day cost to acquire + serve
   one.
2. If profit < cost, you're cash-constrained — add offer roles until it flips (`four-offer-roles`).
3. First target: profit ≥ cost (self-funding). Then target: profit ≥ cost of 2+ more customers.
4. Once acquisition is self-funding or better, scale spend against your capacity to deliver, not
   against a fixed budget.

## Example
A B2B SaaS spends $600 to acquire an account worth $2,400 gross profit over its life, but only
$150 lands in the first 30 days — so every new account is a $450 cash hole. Adding a paid
onboarding package ($500) and an annual-prepay discount moves first-30-day cash to $900, and
acquisition becomes self-funding.

## Pitfalls
- Optimizing lifetime value while ignoring *when* the cash arrives.
- Waiting out a long payback "on faith" without the reserves to survive it.
- Trying to launch with a fully built multi-offer model and no cash — build it in stages
  (`assemble-your-model`).

## Metrics & targets
- 30-day gross profit per customer ≥ 30-day acquisition + service cost (minimum).
- Ideally ≥ 2× that cost.
