# Buy More, Get Some Free (attraction)

## When to use it
Things it makes sense to buy more of. Good for collecting cash up front and extending how long
customers stay.

## The method
Give free units or free time in exchange for buying paid units. The more free value, and the
higher its stated worth, the more people buy — buyers understand price instantly even when they
can't yet judge quality, so a visible "free" is a universally legible deal.

- **Works better with more free than paid.** "Buy 2, get 2 free" pulls harder than "buy 10, get
  2 free."
- **Raise your base prices before you give things away** — permanently and honestly. Since new
  customers enter on this offer, adjust the price to protect margin (at least seasonally). Some
  will still buy at the higher price without the deal, which usefully breaks your own
  pricing assumptions.

## Steps
1. Set the free-to-paid ratio so free ≥ paid in perceived value.
2. Raise base prices to fund the giveaway.
3. Use it to collect cash up front (prepaid blocks) and to lengthen tenure.

## Example
A service business that used to take a year to recover acquisition cost switched to "Buy 6
months, get 6 months free." Far more sign-ups, and the full year's fee collected on day one —
turning a 12-month payback into an immediate one.

## Pitfalls
- Eating the discount instead of raising prices to fund it.
- Using it for something people don't naturally buy in quantity.

## Metrics & targets
- Free component ≥ paid component in stated value.
- Ideal outcome: cash collected up front + longer tenure.
