# Conditional Free Trial (downsell)

## When to use it
When a prospect says no to a recurring product or service, and the customer has to do work to
get results.

## The method
Let them try it free **as long as they meet your terms.** If they complete the required
onboarding actions, they've built the habits of a good long-term customer and usually convert.
If they don't, they pay a penalty fee. This beats an ordinary free trial because people actually
*use* the product.

To run one: **take the card, take the commitment, spell out what they must do (actions +
meetings) to get results, and state the fee if they don't.**

- **Criteria:** reuse the qualifying criteria from a results-based refund offer — attend
  onboarding, do the homework, hit activation milestones.
- **Fee:** break it up by criterion (e.g. $50 per missed item on a $500 fee) or charge one lump
  fee — lump when a single miss really wrecks their outcome.
- Every required onboarding call is also an offer opportunity.

Use a payment plan as the downsell for one-time/physical purchases, and this for recurring ones.
A tiny price ("$1 first month") beats "free" for making sure the card is valid.

## Steps
1. Copy your results-based-refund criteria into the trial terms.
2. Take card + commitment before the trial starts.
3. Design terms that activate and retain; the fee is a backstop, not the goal.

## Example
Workforce software normally charges "$500 onboarding, then $99/month." Trial version: "no
onboarding fee, but you must attend three onboarding calls, complete setup, and activate your
team by the third call — otherwise the $500 applies."

## Pitfalls
- Using it where the customer doesn't need to do work to succeed.
- Making the penalty the objective instead of activation.

## Metrics & targets
- Trials with real terms convert better than plain free trials because usage is higher.
