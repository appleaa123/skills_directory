# Pay Now or Pay Later (attraction)

## When to use it
To pull cash forward and lower acquisition cost, especially for workshops, events, and offers
that benefit from a "free to start" hook.

## The method
Give people two visible options:

- **Pay later:** put a card down for $0, get billed the full price later, and only if satisfied
  (cancel before the charge). This fuses a delayed payment with a satisfaction guarantee, so it
  removes nearly all risk — almost anyone agrees, you can advertise "free," and **you get the
  card on file.**
- **Pay now:** a 20–50% discount plus extra bonuses, paid today. Since the card is already on
  file, it's easy to take.

Whichever they pick, you have a customer and usually some profit. To exploit it fully you need
something else to sell afterward (an upsell).

For a recurring product, the pay-now framing becomes: "lock in the lower rate for good by
starting today; otherwise the rate goes up after 30 days" — plus bonuses.

**Event tactic:** hint at your next offer partway through, not just at the end. It implies
"everyone buys this," which lifts takeup.

## Steps
1. Set the full ("later") price as the anchor.
2. Build the "now" option: 20–50% off + a named bonus stack.
3. Capture the card on the free/pay-later path; follow up to convert to pay-now.
4. Line up an upsell for after the sale.

## Example
An online workshop: **pay later** = $0 now, billed $297 after, cancel before the charge if it
didn't deliver (must attend to qualify); **pay now** = $97 today plus the session recordings as
a bonus. Most choose pay-later, attend, and the charge goes through; a minority take pay-now for
the discount and recordings.

## Pitfalls
- No upsell prepared for after the sale.
- Treating the "later" price as soft — it must be the real, enforced price.

## Metrics & targets
- Pay-now discount: 20–50%, always paired with bonuses.
- The card on file is the hidden prize of the pay-later path.
