# Credit-Forward Offer (upsell)

## When to use it
To re-engage customers who left, to rescue unhappy customers instead of refunding them, to win
over a competitor's unhappy customers, or to upsell regular customers.

## The method
Credit some or all of a customer's previous spend toward your next offer. The prior spend feels
like it would otherwise be wasted, so far more people take the upsell.

Decide three things: how much credit to give, **who** to offer it to, and **how** the credit
rolls over.

Four "who" situations:
1. Customers who lapsed a while ago.
2. Unhappy customers — a better outcome than a cash refund.
3. A competitor's unhappy customers.
4. Regular, satisfied customers.

## Steps
1. Set the credit amount (partial or full prior spend).
2. Pick the audience and the next offer to point the credit at.
3. Offer credit-toward-the-next-thing *before* offering a cash refund.

## Example
A customer asks for a $500 refund. Instead: "I'll credit the full $500 toward a year on the
annual plan." Most take the outcome over the cash. The same move works on a competitor's churned
customer: "bring your last invoice and we'll credit it toward onboarding."

## Pitfalls
- Reaching for cash refunds first — a credited refund keeps both the revenue and the
  relationship.
- Vague credit terms.

## Metrics & targets
- Given the choice between a refund and getting the promised outcome, most people take the
  outcome.
