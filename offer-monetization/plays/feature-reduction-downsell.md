# Feature-Reduction Downsell (downsell)

## When to use it
After a "no" to a prescribed or premium offer. You lower the price by changing *what the
customer gets*.

## The method
Every feature has a price and a value. Remove one → price drops, but so does perceived value.
The *ratio* of price cut to value lost decides whether the buyer sees a better or worse deal —
and **both outcomes produce a sale:**

- Remove something they don't care about, cut the price a lot → **better deal** → they buy the
  reduced offer.
- Remove something they value, cut the price only a little → **worse deal** → they re-sell
  themselves on the original.

So **remove features from highest value to lowest.** People want the most value for their money,
which nudges them toward the highest-value purchase they'll accept.

Formula: **take something away, lower the price, ask "how about now?"**

Ways to reduce: quantity (fewer sessions, shorter term, fewer units), quality (older versions,
cheaper materials), service level (slower response times), or cut an optional component (like
the guarantee).

## Steps
1. List the offer's components by value, highest first.
2. Remove the top item, drop the price, re-present.
3. If they still decline, remove the next one.

## Example
A client declines a package that includes a results guarantee. "If you're comfortable without
the guarantee, I can take $400 off." The guarantee's value (well above $400) becomes visible,
and the client buys the original, guaranteed package.

## Pitfalls
- Stripping so much that the offer stops solving the problem.
- Removing low-value items first — the contrast doesn't teach anything.

## Metrics & targets
- Both "better deal" and "worse deal" reductions convert — one for the downsell, one for the
  original.
