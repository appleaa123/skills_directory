# Guided-Menu Upsell (upsell)

## When to use it
When you have several offers or service tiers available and want to steer the customer to the
right one rather than asking a yes/no question.

## The method
Instead of "do you want to buy this?", tell the customer what they *don't* need and what they
*do* need. "You don't need that — you need this." Four moves:

1. **De-select** — tell them what they don't need, crossing options off. This builds trust and
   focuses attention on what remains.
2. **Prescribe** — tell them what they do need. Explain how it integrates with what they already
   bought, and personalize how to get the most from it. Best when offering a choice would be
   inconvenient and one thing clearly solves the problem.
3. **A or B** — where a choice is useful, ask their preference between two options, not
   yes/no.
4. **Card on file** — remove the last friction: "want to use the card on file?"

## Steps
1. Build the menu of offers/tiers first.
2. For a given customer, decide what to cross off and what to prescribe.
3. Present it as advice, not a pitch; close with A/B and the card on file.

## Example
An agency onboarding call: "You don't need the full done-for-you ad management yet — your
bottleneck is sales follow-up. Start with the sales-training package; here's how it plugs into
the CRM you already have. Do you want the 4-week or 8-week version? I can put it on the card we
have on file."

## Pitfalls
- No real menu to prescribe from.
- Sounding like a salesperson instead of an advisor.
- Skipping the de-select step — it's what earns the trust to prescribe.

## Metrics & targets
- "A or B" outperforms "yes or no" because it assumes the purchase.
