# Commitment Fee Waiver (continuity)

## When to use it
Month-to-month subscription businesses that want longer customer tenure.

## The method
Charge a startup fee to join month-to-month. Then offer to **waive the entire fee if the
customer commits to a term** (e.g. 12 months). If they cancel inside the term, they owe the fee.
People stay longer when leaving costs more than staying.

- **Fee size:** typically 3–5× the monthly rate.
- **The trade:** month-to-month + big fee (flexibility, high cost) vs. term commitment + fee
  waived (commitment, low cost). Most choose to commit and dodge the fee.
- **Risk symmetry:** month-to-month is riskier for you → the fee lowers your risk. A term
  commitment is riskier for them → waiving the fee lowers theirs. If they commit and quit early,
  they simply pay as though they'd been month-to-month all along.

## Steps
1. Set the startup fee at 3–5× the monthly rate.
2. Offer to waive it for a term commitment.
3. Frame the choice as "flexibility costs money, commitment is free."

## Example
Monthly rate $1,000, 12-month term, fee $5,000.
- **Option A:** pay a one-time $5,000 fee + $1,000/month, cancel anytime.
- **Option B:** commit 12 months, fee waived, $1,000/month. Cancel early → owe the $5,000.
Most take Option B.

## Pitfalls
- Setting the fee so high it kills month-to-month sign-ups you actually want (sometimes fine —
  know which you're optimizing for).
- Not making the early-cancellation math explicit up front.

## Metrics & targets
- Startup fee ≈ 3–5× monthly.
- Early cancellation just reverts them to month-to-month economics — not a loss.
