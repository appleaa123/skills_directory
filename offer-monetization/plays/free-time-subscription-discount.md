# Free-Time Subscription Discount (continuity)

## When to use it
When you want term commitments on a subscription. Tune the mechanics to your churn history.

## The method
Give the customer free subscription time — now or later — for signing up today.

**Where to apply the discount** (each trades conversion vs. churn vs. cash):
- **Up front:** free time first, the paid term starts after. Converts more, but delays cash and
  can raise churn. Only for categories that enforce contracts well.
- **At the end:** they *earn* bonus free time by making every payment on time. Fewer sign-ups,
  lower churn.
- **Spread across the term:** e.g. 3 months free on a 1-year deal = the discount value ÷ 12 off
  each month. Keeps cash flowing while giving the full discount.
- **After the first 1–2 payments:** collect a little cash first (framed as "first and last
  month" or an activation fee), then apply the one-time discount. Also confirms the card works.

**Extend the term, don't eat into it:** "3 months free on a 1-year sign-up" should mean *pay 12,
get 15 total* — not *pay 9, get 12*.

**Cancellation:** prefer that customers cancel by *paying back the discount they received*,
which returns them to the month-to-month rate. Always make cancellation instructions clear;
offer an exit interview (waive the cancellation fee for it) — you'll often save or upsell them.

## Steps
1. Check your churn history: high churn → backload or earn; low churn → frontload is safer.
2. Pick where the discount lands.
3. Structure the term as an extension, and make cancel = repay the discount.

## Two extra levers
- **Bill every 4 weeks instead of monthly.** A year has 13 four-week cycles, not 12 → about
  +8.3% revenue for the same customers and the same work. At 20% margins that's roughly +41%
  annual profit.
- **Gift cards:** deliver the discount as a gift card — the buyer pays full price and can gift
  it or redeem it later (full-price sign-up plus a referral).

## Example
"$399/month. Sign up today and get your first 30 days free" (frontloaded) — or, for a
high-churn business: "make all 12 payments on time and month 13 is free" (earned). Billed as
"$399 every 4 weeks."

## Pitfalls
- Frontloading discounts when churn is already high.
- Letting the discount shorten the paid term instead of extending the total.

## Metrics & targets
- Frontload = more sign-ups, more churn. Backload = fewer sign-ups, less churn. Spread = cash
  keeps flowing.
