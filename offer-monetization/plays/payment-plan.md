# Payment Plan (downsell)

## When to use it
When a prospect says "I can't afford it." Best for one-time purchases and physical products.
Same total price — they pay some now and the rest over time.

## The method
Don't accept "can't afford it" at face value — restructure the ask. Descend a ladder until you
find a structure they'll say yes to:

full payment → half now / half on payday → thirds → smaller installments → "when *can* you pay,
and how much?"

Anchor the schedule to the customer's own cash flow ("when do you get paid?"), not a generic
calendar. Collect the largest deposit you can.

The follow-up payment conversations are themselves opportunities to offer more.

A payment plan is a gamble — some cards fail. It only grows the business if it brings in *more*
customers than you'd otherwise get; treat breakage as a cost of doing business.

## Steps
1. When you hear "can't afford it," ask "when do you get paid?"
2. Offer half now / half then; if not, descend the ladder.
3. Time the charges to their pay dates.
4. Take the biggest deposit you can get.

## Example
A $2,000 program, prospect says no. "When do you get paid? … The 1st? Put $500 down today and
$500 on the 1st for three months." Still no. "What can you do?" — "I can pay it all on the 1st."
"Great, card now, I'll run it on the 2nd."

## Pitfalls
- Arguing with the objection instead of restructuring it.
- A zero or tiny deposit — it hurts 30-day cash and raises breakage risk.

## Metrics & targets
- Collect a meaningful deposit to protect 30-day payback.
- Factor card breakage into pricing.
