# Glossary — Offer Design

**All-sales-final guarantee** — an explicit no-refund policy paired with a credible reason;
used for consumables or items that lose value once delivered.

**Bonus stack** — the deliverables of an offer broken into named, individually-priced
components and presented together, with a summed value well above the price.

**Category of one** — a market position where no clean comparison to an alternative is
possible, so price is set by perceived value.

**Commodity** — anything a buyer can get from many sources; bought on price, not value.

**Conditional guarantee** — a refund or remedy contingent on the buyer completing agreed
actions; also used to drive results.

**Confidence it will work** — the buyer's belief the offer will produce the result for them;
one of the four perceived-value drivers. Raised by proof, track record, and guarantees.

**Cut and bundle** — removing low-value/high-cost deliverables and packaging the high-value/
low-cost ones into a single priced offer.

**Delivery method** — how a solution is provided: group size, done-for-you vs. do-it-yourself,
access level, format, speed.

**Desired result** — the outcome the buyer actually wants; one of the four perceived-value
drivers. Sell the destination, not the vehicle.

**Differentiated offer** — an offer combining a promotion, an unmatched value proposition, a
premium price, a strong guarantee, and acquisition-funding payment terms so it can't be compared.

**Effort required** — everything the buyer must do or give up; one of the four perceived-value
drivers. Lower it to raise value.

**Gross profit** — revenue minus the direct cost of serving one additional customer.

**Lifetime value** — gross profit per purchase × number of purchases per customer.

**Obstacle list** — every point of friction and limiting belief a buyer hits before, during,
and after purchase; the raw material for building an offer.

**Offer** — what you provide, how payment is taken, and the terms of the deal.

**Offer fatigue** — decline in response after long use of the same offer; fixed by renaming.

**Performance-based guarantee** — you're paid on results (revenue share, per-outcome, bonuses/
penalties).

**Perceived-value formula** — value ≈ (desired result × confidence) ÷ (time to result × effort).

**Premium pricing** — pricing against the buyer's outcome at a large multiple of delivery cost.

**Price-to-value gap** — the distance between price and perceived value; widen it by adding
value, not cutting price.

**Retired offer / price** — permanently discontinuing an offer or price point to create real
scarcity.

**Rolling cohort** — buyers start in batches on a fixed cadence, creating a truthful recurring
deadline.

**Sales-vs-delivery balance** — doing more makes an offer easier to sell and harder to deliver,
and vice versa; aim for the point that does both well.

**Scarcity** — limiting quantity to raise willingness to pay.

**Time to result** — the wait between paying and getting the outcome (and until first visible
progress); one of the four perceived-value drivers. Lower it.

**Three ways to grow** — more customers, higher average purchase value, more purchases per
customer.

**Unconditional guarantee** — a no-questions refund within a window; effectively a paid trial.

**Urgency** — limiting time (a deadline, a cohort) to force a decision now.

**Virtuous cycle of price** — higher price → more buyer commitment → better results → better
proof → easier premium sales → more margin to reinvest.
