# Patterns — Offer Design

Each method as a compact, reusable play: when to use · how · trade-offs.

## Market selection (4 indicators + leverage order)
**When:** before building the offer, or when nothing works.
**How:** score candidate markets on acute pain, ability to pay, easy to reach, growing. Drop any
that fail pain/budget/reach or are shrinking. Remember: market demand > offer strength >
persuasion skill. Niche into a growing sub-group of health / wealth / relationships and commit.
**Trade-offs:** narrower makes more money until the niche's size becomes the ceiling.

## Escape price competition
**When:** buyers compare you like-for-like; margins thin.
**How:** identify the exact comparison points, then change enough of them (scope, delivery model,
guarantee, terms, name) that the comparison breaks. Lead with outcome and terms.
**Trade-offs:** requires real differences buyers care about, not cosmetic ones.

## The 5-part differentiated offer
**When:** building the anchor offer your business runs on.
**How:** attractive promotion + unmatched value proposition + premium price + strong guarantee +
acquisition-funding payment terms.
**Trade-offs:** the value core must be strong first; enhancement multiplies, it doesn't create.

## Premium pricing + virtuous cycle
**When:** setting or raising a price; thin margins.
**How:** price against the buyer's concrete outcome, at a large multiple of delivery cost. Add
proof + guarantee. When raising an existing price, add value at the same time.
**Trade-offs:** needs proof and a guarantee to hold; low prices attract low-commitment buyers.

## Perceived-value diagnosis
**When:** any offer feels weak or won't convert.
**How:** score desired result / confidence / time to result / effort; fix the weakest. Usually
the denominator (speed, effort) has the most upside.
**Trade-offs:** raising confidence via guarantees can attract guarantee-only buyers — tie to
required actions.

## Build the offer (5 steps)
**When:** creating or rebuilding an offer.
**How:** name the outcome → list every obstacle in sequence → convert each to a "how to"
solution → brainstorm delivery methods → cut low-value/high-cost, bundle high-value/low-cost
with stated values above price.
**Trade-offs:** over-deliver on v1 to make sales and learn; systematize later without changing
the promise.

## Scarcity
**When:** to support premium pricing and lift willingness to pay.
**How:** limited slots (cap intake, announce fills), limited bonuses, retired-for-good offers,
limited physical runs.
**Trade-offs:** must be genuinely enforced; fake scarcity permanently damages trust.

## Urgency
**When:** deals stall without a decision.
**How:** rolling cohorts (truthful recurring deadline + choreographed onboarding), real seasonal
promotions, announced price/bonus deadlines, genuinely closing opportunities.
**Trade-offs:** never move a deadline once set.

## Bonus stacking
**When:** presenting or closing; want to close without discounting.
**How:** anchor to core price, add named/priced bonuses each solving the next obstacle, each
with a reason; total value far above price; favor tools/templates/checklists. One-to-one: ask
for the sale first, reveal bonuses after yes.
**Trade-offs:** too many bonuses bury the core offer.

## Guarantee construction
**When:** always.
**How:** "If not [result] in [timeframe], then [remedy]." Pick type by delivery cost:
unconditional (low cost, max buyers), conditional / better-than-money-back (default, drives
results), all-sales-final (consumables/high cost), performance-based (aligns incentives). Run
the math, not the emotion.
**Trade-offs:** bold guarantees lift conversion more than refunds — but guarantee-only buyers
underperform.

## Offer naming (5-part checklist)
**When:** naming an offer, writing a headline, refreshing a faded offer.
**How:** combine 3–5 of reason / audience / result / timeframe / format. Keep it short. Test
written variants. Rename (don't rebuild) to reset a faded offer.
**Trade-offs:** all five can make the name unwieldy.

## "Generate demand, then add friction"
**When:** launching anything.
**How:** create demand first, get people to yes by over-delivering, and only then trim the offer
or add marketing friction. Don't optimize before you have cash flow and feedback.
