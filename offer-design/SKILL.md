# Offer Design

An operating playbook for building offers people buy quickly and at a premium. The offer — what
you provide, what it costs, and the terms — is the first thing a prospect reacts to, so it
decides more of your economics than the delivery work does.

## When to use this

- Prospects say you're expensive or compare you line-for-line with cheaper options
- Conversion is low even with steady traffic or leads
- Margins are thin despite a full client load
- You're launching a new product, entering a market, or repricing
- You want to raise prices and keep (or grow) volume
- Response to an offer that used to work is fading

## The core model

**1. Market beats offer beats persuasion.** A strong-demand market with a mediocre offer
outperforms a weak-demand market with a great offer. Pick the market first; a great offer aimed
at people who don't urgently need it falls flat. → `plays/market-selection.md`

**2. Escape comparison.** When a buyer can line you up against a near-identical option, the
cheaper one wins by default and your price gets ground down to survival margins. The fix is
almost never cutting cost — it's making the offer hard to compare. → `plays/escape-price-competition.md`,
`plays/differentiated-offer.md`

**3. Price to the outcome, not your cost.** Aim for a large gap between what delivery costs you
and what you charge. A higher price also raises perceived value and buyer commitment, which
improves results and testimonials. → `plays/premium-pricing.md`

**4. Perceived value is a ratio:**

> value ≈ (**desired result** × **confidence it will work**) ÷ (**time to result** × **effort required**)

Move all four levers the right way. The denominator usually matters most — people pay for speed
and for having the work taken off their hands. → `plays/perceived-value.md`

**5. Build the offer from the buyer's problem list.** Name the outcome they want → list every
obstacle before, during, and after the purchase → turn each obstacle into a deliverable →
brainstorm ways to deliver each → cut the low-value/high-cost items and bundle the rest with
stated values well above price. → `plays/build-the-offer.md`

**6. Enhance it.** Add limited quantity (scarcity), a real deadline (urgency), a stack of named
bonuses, a risk-reversing guarantee, and a name that signals the outcome and timeframe.
→ `plays/scarcity-and-urgency.md`, `plays/bonus-stacking.md`, `plays/guarantees.md`, `plays/naming-offers.md`

## Build sequence

Market → desired outcome → obstacle list → deliverables → cut & bundle → scarcity → urgency →
bonuses → guarantee → name.

## Scope

This is a general operating playbook for offer structuring, pricing, and direct-response
conversion. It assumes you already have a real product or service and some way to reach buyers
(for that, see `customer-acquisition`; for sequencing multiple offers to fund growth, see
`offer-monetization`). Numeric guidance is a starting heuristic — validate against your own
data.
