# Guarantees and Risk Reversal

## When to use it
Always — perceived risk that "it won't work for me" is the single biggest objection to any
purchase. Reversing it is the fastest way to lift conversion.

## The method
A guarantee needs teeth. The structure is:

> If you do not get **[specific result]** within **[timeframe]**, we will **[specific remedy]**.

Without the "or else" part, a guarantee is weak. "We'll get you results" is vague; "20 qualified
leads in 30 days or your money back plus your ad spend" has teeth.

### Four types

| Type | Structure | Best for |
|---|---|---|
| **Unconditional** | No-questions refund within a window; effectively a paid trial | Low delivery cost; maximizes buyers; expect some refunds |
| **Conditional** | Refund or remedy *only if* the buyer did the agreed actions | Default choice; also drives results — bake in the actions that make buyers succeed. Aim for "better than money back" (extra work free, double your money, a bonus) |
| **All-sales-final** | Explicitly no refunds, with a credible reason why (show a real cost/exposure on your side) | Consumables, or things that lose value once delivered |
| **Performance-based** | You're paid on results — revenue share, profit share, per-outcome, bonuses/penalties | Aligning incentives; services where you can influence the outcome |

You can combine them (e.g. unconditional for 30 days, then conditional).

### The math, not the emotion
A bolder guarantee lifts conversion more than it lifts refunds. If you close 30% more buyers and
refunds double from 5% to 10%, you still net ~1.23× the revenue — all to the bottom line. A
guarantee only fails to pay off if the extra sales are almost entirely cancelled out by extra
refunds.

If your delivery cost is high, prefer conditional or all-sales-final — you'd otherwise eat both
the refund and the delivery cost.

## Steps
1. Pick the guarantee type from your delivery-cost structure (see the table).
2. Write it as "If not [specific result] in [timeframe], then [specific remedy]."
3. Make the remedy "better than money back" where you can (extra work free, a bonus, double
   back).
4. Fold the buyer's required actions into the terms, so the guarantee also drives results.
5. Sanity-check the economics: estimate the conversion lift vs. the refund-rate lift.
6. State it boldly, with a reason why, everywhere the offer appears.

## Example
An agency moved from "we'll grow your pipeline" (no teeth) to "20 booked sales calls in your
first 30 days, or we work free until you get them, plus we refund that month" — a conditional,
better-than-money-back guarantee with the client's required inputs written into the terms.
Conversion roughly doubled; most clients hit the number and never invoked it.

## Pitfalls
- Buyers who purchase *only* because of the guarantee often make poor customers — tie the
  guarantee to the work they must do.
- A guarantee with no specific result or timeframe.
- Offering an unconditional refund on a high-cost-to-deliver service.

## Metrics & targets
- Always state a guarantee boldly, with a reason, even a modest one.
- Prefer "better than money back" to "money back."
- Match the guarantee type to your delivery-cost structure.
