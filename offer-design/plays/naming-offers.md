# Naming Offers

## When to use it
Naming a new offer, writing the headline for an ad or landing page, or refreshing an offer whose
response has faded.

## The method
The name decides whether the right person is interested enough to act. Build it from up to five
components — use three to five, keep it short, and test written variants:

1. **Reason** — why this offer exists now ("free," "40% off," "spring," "new location,"
   "anniversary"). It answers "why are they doing this / what's in it for me?" It can be almost
   anything as long as it's true.
2. **Audience** — call out exactly who it's for, as specifically as you can ("independent
   pharmacy owners," "Austin moms," "B2B SaaS founders"). Hyper-local beats city-level for local
   businesses.
3. **Result** — the desired outcome, stated concretely ("pain-free," "first 10 clients,"
   "tax-ready books").
4. **Timeframe** — how long to the result ("in 30 days," "6-week," "90-day").
5. **Format** — the container word ("challenge," "bootcamp," "intensive," "sprint," "program,"
   "blueprint").

### Refreshing a faded offer
Offers wear out over years (faster in small local markets). When response fades, **rename it** —
you're changing the wrapper, not the offer. Rotate the creative, hooks, and stories around the
same offer first; rename when that's exhausted. Note: reaching an audience once is not fatigue —
most people don't register an offer on the first exposure.

## Steps
1. Write out the five components for your offer: reason, audience, result, timeframe, format.
2. Draft 5–10 names, each combining 3–5 of the components; keep every one short.
3. Cut anything that hides the result or the timeframe.
4. Test the top candidates as live headlines and keep the one that pulls.
5. For a faded offer: exhaust new creative and hooks first, then rename (don't rebuild).

## Example
"Free 6-Week Tax-Ready Sprint for Independent Pharmacy Owners" —
reason (free), timeframe (6-week), result (tax-ready), format (sprint), audience (independent
pharmacy owners). All five, still short.

## Pitfalls
- Cramming all five in until the name is unwieldy — brevity vs. specificity is a balance.
- A clever name that hides the outcome.
- Renaming when the real problem is a weak offer or the wrong market.

## Metrics & targets
- 3–5 components, short and punchy.
- Front the result and the timeframe — those are what pull.
- The only reliable test is writing several and trying them.
