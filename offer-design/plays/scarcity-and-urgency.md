# Scarcity and Urgency

## When to use it
Deals stall without a decision. Prospects say "let me think about it" and disappear. You want to
support a premium price. Apply only on top of an already-good offer.

## The method
Two distinct levers:

- **Scarcity = limited quantity.** When supply is (or genuinely appears) lower than demand,
  willingness to pay rises. People want what few others can have.
- **Urgency = limited time.** A real deadline forces a decision now instead of "later" (which
  usually means never).

They stack well ("5 spots, closes Friday"), but keep the concepts separate.

### Ethical scarcity tactics (for services)
1. **Limited slots** — cap total clients, or clients accepted per period ("5 new clients a
   month"). Announce when spots fill.
2. **Limited bonuses** — the core offer stays available, but a specific bonus is only for the
   first N buyers or until a date.
3. **Retired for good** — permanently discontinue an offer or a price ("once this closes, it
   doesn't come back").
4. **Physical goods** — limited runs, limited editions, seasonal drops.

### Ethical urgency mechanisms
1. **Rolling cohorts** — buyers start in batches on a fixed cadence. "Enroll by Friday or wait
   for the next cohort." Bonus: a repeatable, choreographed onboarding.
2. **Seasonal promotions** — real promotions tied to real dates, with a real end.
3. **Price or bonus deadline** — "price goes up Monday" or "this bonus ends at the deadline."
   Always announce price increases in advance.
4. **Genuinely closing opportunity** — the opportunity itself decays (inventory, a market
   window, an arbitrage). Explain the real mechanism.

## Steps
1. Confirm the underlying offer is strong — enhancement can't rescue a weak one.
2. Pick one scarcity tactic and one urgency mechanism that are *structurally true* for your
   business.
3. Make the limit visible and specific (a number, a date).
4. Enforce it exactly once and it stays credible forever; move it once and it's worthless.

## Example
A consultancy caps onboarding at 4 new clients per quarter and runs enrollment in monthly
cohorts. Both limits are real (delivery capacity, onboarding logistics), so "2 spots left,
cohort closes the 28th" is honest and it consistently converts fence-sitters.

## Pitfalls
- Fake countdowns and phantom "only 1 left" claims — they work until a buyer notices, then they
  poison trust permanently.
- A deadline you extend "just this once."
- Adding scarcity/urgency to an offer people don't want anyway.

## Metrics & targets
- If you'd be uncomfortable having the scarcity claim audited, don't make it.
- Rolling cohorts give you a truthful deadline every single cycle.
