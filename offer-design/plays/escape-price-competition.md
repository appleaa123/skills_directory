# Escape Price Competition

## When to use it
Prospects compare you directly to alternatives, ask "what's your price?" before "what do I get?",
or switch to a cheaper provider offering "the same thing." Margins are being ground down.

## The method
A commodity is anything a buyer can get from many places. When your thing looks equivalent to
another thing, the cheaper one wins by default, and competition drives everyone's margins down
to just enough to keep the lights on. Cutting your price to compete accelerates the race to the
bottom.

The escape is to make the offer **hard to compare** — bundle, reframe, reprice, and add terms
so that the buyer is choosing between your offer and nothing, not your offer and a rival's. When
there's no clean comparison, price is set by the value the buyer perceives, not by the next
option on the list.

There are only three ways to grow revenue:
1. more customers,
2. higher average purchase value,
3. more purchases per customer.

An offer that can't be compared helps all three at once: more people respond, more of them buy,
and they buy at higher prices.

## Steps
1. Write down exactly how a prospect would compare you to the nearest alternative. Those
   comparison points are your problem.
2. Change enough of them that the comparison breaks: different scope, different delivery model,
   different guarantee, different payment terms, a name that isn't the category name.
3. Lead with the outcome and the terms, not the deliverable list.
4. Stop explaining why you're different — let the structure of the offer make that obvious.

## Example
A bookkeeping firm priced at "$500/month, same as everyone." Repackaged as "books closed by the
5th every month or that month is free, plus a quarterly cash-flow review and a tax-ready year-end
package — one fixed annual fee." Same underlying work; no longer a like-for-like line item, so
the monthly-rate comparison stops happening.

## Pitfalls
- Competing on price "just this once" to land a client — it teaches them, and everyone they
  talk to, that your price is negotiable.
- Differentiating on something buyers don't care about.
- Assuming the buyer will do the work of understanding why you're better. If they have to ask,
  they usually won't.

## Metrics & targets
- Track gross profit (revenue minus the direct cost of serving one more customer) and lifetime
  value (gross profit per purchase × purchases per customer), not just top-line revenue.
- A market that has driven margins to survival level is telling you it's fully commoditized —
  differentiate or leave.
