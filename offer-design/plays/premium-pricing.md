# Premium Pricing

## When to use it
Setting or raising a price. Margins are thin. You suspect you're leaving money on the table, or
you're nervous about charging more.

## The method
Never charge more than the offer is worth to the buyer — but charge far more than it costs you
to deliver. Aim for a large multiple of cost, not a small markup. If the value is real, a high
price is still a good deal for the buyer.

Price against the buyer's outcome, not your inputs and not your comfort level. When the value is
made concrete ("this adds roughly $X for you; it costs $Y"), the price objection tends to
dissolve.

A higher price also drives a **virtuous cycle**:

higher price → the buyer is more invested → they do the work and get better results → better
testimonials and referrals → easier to sell the next buyer at a premium → more margin to
reinvest in the experience → repeat.

Low prices do the opposite: they attract low-commitment buyers who don't show up or do the work,
which produces weak results and weak proof.

## Steps
1. Estimate the concrete value of the outcome to a typical buyer (revenue gained, cost avoided,
   time saved, pain removed).
2. Set the price at a fraction of that value that still leaves the buyer clearly ahead — and a
   large multiple of your delivery cost.
3. Build in the proof and guarantee that justify it (`perceived-value`, `guarantees`).
4. When you raise prices on an existing offer, add value or a bonus at the same time so the
   change reads as an upgrade, not a penalty.
5. Hold the price. If you must move on a deal, add bonuses rather than discount (`bonus-stacking`).

## Example
A coaching program that helped clients add roughly $200k in annual revenue was priced at $3k and
converting poorly — the low price signaled low value. Repriced at $30k with a results guarantee
and a concrete "here's the math" pitch: fewer leads, far more revenue, and clients who actually
implemented because they had skin in the game.

## Pitfalls
- Pricing off your costs or off what feels polite.
- Discounting to close — it trains buyers that the price is soft and starves the experience you
  can fund.
- Raising the price with nothing added to explain the change.

## Metrics & targets
- Target price ≈ a large multiple of delivery cost (think 5–10×+ where the value supports it),
  not 2–3×.
- If completion rates and results are low, a too-low price is a likely cause.
