# The Perceived-Value Formula

## When to use it
To pressure-test any offer, diagnose why one isn't converting, or decide which improvement to
make next.

## The method
Perceived value is a ratio of four drivers:

> value ≈ ( **desired result** × **confidence it will work** ) ÷ ( **time to result** × **effort required** )

- **Desired result (raise it):** the gap between where the buyer is and where they want to be.
  Sell the destination, not the vehicle. Make it vivid and specific.
- **Confidence it will work (raise it):** the buyer's belief that it will work *for them*. Built
  with proof, case studies, credentials, track record, risk reversal, and guarantees. If this is
  near zero, nothing else matters — no one buys a result they don't believe they'll get.
- **Time to result (lower it):** the wait between paying and getting the outcome — and, just as
  important, the wait until the *first* visible progress. Shorten it, or engineer fast early wins
  so it feels fast.
- **Effort required (lower it):** everything the buyer has to do or give up. Every piece of
  effort you remove — done-for-you instead of do-it-yourself, tools, templates, accountability,
  scheduling — raises value.

The **denominator usually wins.** People pay the most for speed and for having the work taken
off their hands. A premium is largely "skip the time and skip the mistakes."

## Steps
1. Score each of the four drivers for your current offer (weak / okay / strong).
2. Fix the weakest one first.
3. For that driver, list concrete offer changes that improve it, then fold the best into the
   offer (`build-the-offer`).
4. Re-score and repeat.

## Example
"Six-week program to lower your stress" beats "float-tank session" for the same service: it
names the result (less stress), gives a timeframe (six weeks), implies a structured path, and —
if offered free or low-cost as an entry point — removes price friction. Three of the four
drivers move in your favor from the name alone.

## Pitfalls
- Piling on features (raising effort to consume) when the fix is to remove effort.
- Selling the deliverable ("12 coaching calls") instead of the result.
- Ignoring confidence — a big promise with no proof reads as a scam.

## Metrics & targets
- Diagnose with the four drivers, not gut feel.
- Speed and effort-removal are usually the highest-leverage levers to pull.
