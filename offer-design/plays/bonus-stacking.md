# Bonus Stacking

## When to use it
Presenting or closing an offer, especially when you want to close without discounting, or widen
the gap between price and perceived value.

## The method
The same set of deliverables is worth more when it's broken into named, individually-priced
components and presented as a stack than when it's described as one lump. Until you enumerate
what you do, the buyer doesn't credit you for it.

Mechanics:
- **Anchor to the core price first**, then add bonuses so the value gap widens with each one.
  You're increasing value delivered, not cutting price.
- **Name every bonus** and give it a **specific dollar value**, ideally tied to a price you'd
  really charge.
- **Give each bonus a reason** it exists and why it's included now.
- Each bonus should **solve the buyer's next problem** (from your obstacle list).
- Favor bonuses that are **tools, checklists, templates, scripts, or a faster path** — high
  perceived value, low cost and time for you, and they lower the buyer's effort.
- The **summed value of the bonuses should far exceed the price.**

Where bonuses come from: things you already do but haven't itemized; solutions to downstream
problems; assets you've already built; partner products you can include.

**Add bonuses instead of discounting.** Discounting signals weakness and teaches buyers your
price is negotiable. Adding value to close signals strength and goodwill.

In a one-to-one sale, ask for the sale first; reveal bonuses *after* they've said yes, as a
delight.

## Steps
1. From your obstacle list, pick the 3–6 problems buyers hit right after purchase.
2. Turn each into a deliverable you already have or can make cheaply (template, checklist,
   script, faster path).
3. Name each one and put a real dollar value on it, tied to a price you'd actually charge.
4. Write a one-line "reason why" each bonus is included.
5. Present: state the core offer and its price first, then stack the bonuses on top; show the
   summed value against the price.
6. Never discount the core offer to close — add another bonus instead.

## Example
A $4,000 group program, presented as: the core program ($4,000 value) + a private onboarding
call ($500) + a templates library ($750) + a 90-day accountability channel ($1,000) + a
quick-start guide for results in week one ($250). Stated value $6,500, price $4,000. Each bonus
maps to a specific reason people stall.

## Pitfalls
- Bonuses that don't relate to the buyer's real next problem — filler dilutes the stack.
- So many bonuses the core offer gets lost.
- Discounting the core offer to close instead of adding a bonus.

## Metrics & targets
- Total bonus value should clearly dwarf the price.
- Every bonus: named, priced, with a stated reason.
